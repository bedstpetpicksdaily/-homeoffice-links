Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Sturdy electric motor with dual-motor stability: The desk uses a dual-motor system that handles weight distribution better than budget single-motor alternatives. Users report minimal wobble even at maximum height extension, which matters if you're placing monitors, lamps, and office equipment on top.
  • ✓ Bamboo or laminate surface options: The bamboo option genuinely looks better than particle board alternatives and doesn't feel cheap. It's a small detail that affects your daily experience, especially if your desk is visible during video calls.
  • ✓ Programmable height presets: You get four memory buttons to save your preferred sitting and standing heights. No more manually adjusting every transition. This actually increases the likelihood you'll alternate positions throughout the day instead of just standing for 30 minutes then giving up.
  • ✓ Reasonable footprint for tight spaces: At approximately 48-60 inches wide depending on configuration, the Cooper doesn't demand a massive room. It's narrower than many commercial standing desks, which matters if your home office is genuinely a corner, not a dedicated room.
Cons
  • ✗ Price doesn't clearly justify the jump over budget alternatives: At its current price range, you're paying 40-60% more than basic Amazon standing desks with similar motor specs. The Fully brand name carries weight in the industry, but home buyers don't always benefit from that premium the way corporate offices do. A no-name dual-motor desk delivers nearly identical functionality for significantly less.
  • ✗ Wobble and stability concerns reported by heavier users: Several reviews mention noticeable desk sway when the surface is fully extended and loaded with equipment, particularly above 45 inches height. If you're placing multiple monitors and a heavy lamp on top, this becomes a real problem during typing.

Our Verdict

The Fully Cooper Electric Standing Desk is competent but overpriced for what most home office workers actually need. At its current price point, you're paying for the brand reputation and slightly better build quality than the absolute cheapest options, but you're not getting dramatically superior performance. The 4.3-star rating reflects a desk that works fine for typical use—sitting, standing, a couple of monitors. If you have disposable income and value the peace of mind that comes with buying from an established brand, go ahead. If you're budget-conscious (and you should be), spend half the price on a no-name dual-motor desk from Amazon and use the savings on a quality monitor arm or ergonomic chair, which will actually impact your comfort more. The Cooper isn't a bad desk. It's just not special enough to justify the cost when you're buying for yourself, not outfitting an office.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Fully Cooper worth the price compared to cheaper standing desks?

Not really, unless brand reputation matters to you. The dual-motor system and bamboo surface are nice, but you can get the same core functionality (electric adjustment, preset buttons, stable frame) from lesser-known brands for $200-300 less. The real question: would you rather have the Fully Cooper or that same Fully Cooper budget plus an $300 Herman Miller chair? The chair will change your comfort far more noticeably than a slightly nicer desk surface.

Does the bamboo surface justify the extra cost?

Partially. Bamboo looks and feels significantly better than particle board, and it's more durable. You'll notice this daily. However, laminate options exist that look nearly identical to bamboo for $100+ less. If you're visible on video calls frequently and aesthetics matter to you, the bamboo upgrade is defensible. If nobody sees your desk, save your money.

What's the real stability like when standing and typing?

Stable at most heights, but not perfect. The dual-motor design minimizes wobble compared to single-motor desks, and users consistently praise this. However, some heavier users (220+ lbs) report noticeable movement when the desk is fully extended and heavily loaded. Test the height range you'll actually use. If you're staying under 45 inches or only placing a monitor and keyboard on top, you won't notice the wobble. If you're stacking equipment, this becomes relevant.
