Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Anker 777 work with Windows laptops, or just MacBooks?

It technically works with Windows machines that have native Thunderbolt 3 ports, but most consumer Windows laptops marketed as 'USB-C' actually lack true Thunderbolt implementation. Dell XPS 13 Plus (2024+) and ASUS ProArt series support it natively. Before purchasing, check your laptop's specifications for 'Thunderbolt 3' or 'Thunderbolt 4'—USB 3.2 is not equivalent. If you're on a budget Windows machine with standard USB-C, a universal docking station is a better investment.

Is 96W charging enough for my laptop, or will it throttle performance?

96W handles most laptops up to 16 inches without thermal throttling during normal work. I tested with sustained video editing rendering—the dock delivered consistent power. However, if you're running both a 16-inch MacBook Pro and external GPU simultaneously while gaming or doing 3D rendering, you might hit power limits. For typical office work, presentations, and content creation, it's sufficient. Check your laptop's rated power requirement; if it exceeds 96W, this dock isn't designed for your use case.

How does the 777 compare to universal USB-C docks that cost $100 less?

The primary difference is Thunderbolt 3's 40Gbps bandwidth versus USB 3.1's 10Gbps. If you're transferring files occasionally and working with cloud storage, a $100 USB-C dock is fine. If you're managing local video editing, external storage, or multiple high-bandwidth peripherals, the speed difference compounds quickly—you'll recover the $100 difference in saved time within months. The 777 also tends toward better build quality and thermal management in my testing compared to budget alternatives that run hot under load.
