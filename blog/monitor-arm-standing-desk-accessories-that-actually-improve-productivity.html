What to Look For

  • Full Range of Motion with Easy Adjustments — Your monitor arm should allow smooth height, tilt, and swivel adjustments without tools. Since you're transitioning between sitting and standing throughout the day, you need an arm that adapts instantly. Look for gas spring mechanisms rather than friction-based systems; they'll hold your monitor exactly where you position it without drifting.
  • Weight Capacity That Matches Your Setup — Don't just check if your single monitor fits. Consider whether you might add a second screen later, or if you're running a larger ultra-wide display. A monitor arm rated for 20-30 lbs gives you flexibility without the wobbly instability of an undersized mount. This matters because monitor movement creates neck strain and breaks focus.
  • Desk Space Preservation — A clamp-based or grommet-mounted arm keeps your actual desktop real estate free for keyboard, mouse, notebooks, or coffee. This is especially critical in standing desk setups where surface area is already limited and you need quick access to peripherals at different heights.
