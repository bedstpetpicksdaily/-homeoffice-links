Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Series 1 compare to Herman Miller Mirra 2 or Autonomous Ergo Pro?

Mirra 2 costs 2.5x more and gives you more adjustability—better for people with complex ergonomic needs. Autonomous Ergo Pro is closer in price but relies on tilt-tensioning (harder to dial in precisely). Series 1 splits the difference: less premium than Mirra but more reliable out of the box than cheaper competitors. If budget matters, Series 1 wins. If you need maximum customization, Mirra 2 justifies the premium.

Will this chair work for someone 6'3" or 5'2"?

Tall people (6'+) report this works if you don't have long legs—the seat height max is around 21 inches, which is adequate but not generous. Under 5'6", you'll find it comfortable. The four-way adjustable armrests help both extremes more than the seat depth does. If you're outlier-tall, look at chairs specifically marketed for 6'3"+.

How long before the mesh sags or tears?

Steelcase's mesh is industrial-grade, not that thin fabric other brands use. Users report three to five years before any visible sagging occurs. Tears are rare unless you're doing something aggressive. The real wear point is usually the armrest padding (around year three), not the mesh back itself.

Is assembly a nightmare?

Assembly takes 20-25 minutes if you have basic tools and follow the steps. The main frustration: casters sometimes arrive slightly loose from shipping. Tighten them immediately after assembly or they'll wobble. Steelcase includes an allen wrench and the instructions are actually clear—this is one area where they didn't cut corners.

Does the chair support weight over 250 pounds consistently?

Steelcase rates Series 1 for up to 275 pounds. Reviews from people in that range mention some cushion compression around year two, but nothing catastrophic. If you're 280+, monitor reviews from users your weight—some report better longevity than others depending on daily usage hours.
