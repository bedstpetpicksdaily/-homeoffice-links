These items address the primary concerns of standing desk users: screen positioning, wrist strain prevention, foot comfort, and workspace organization. The Full Explanation

Monitor Arm

A monitor arm is arguably the most important standing desk accessory. When standing, your monitor should be positioned at eye level approximately 20-26 inches from your face. An adjustable monitor arm allows you to achieve this position without relying on books or makeshift stands. Look for arms with full articulation—tilt, swivel, and height adjustment—so you can customize the position throughout your day. Quality monitor arms also free up valuable desk space by mounting your display vertically.

Ergonomic Keyboard and Mouse

Standing desk users experience higher rates of wrist strain compared to seated workers, making an ergonomic keyboard and mouse critical investments. An ergonomic keyboard keeps your wrists in a neutral position, reducing repetitive strain injuries. Pair this with a vertical mouse or trackball that positions your hand in a natural handshake position rather than a pronated (palm-down) position. These tools significantly reduce fatigue and discomfort during extended standing periods.

Anti-Fatigue Mat

Standing all day on hard flooring creates foot and leg fatigue, even with proper posture. An anti-fatigue mat provides cushioning and encourages subtle micro-movements that improve circulation and reduce strain on your feet and lower back. Quality anti-fatigue mats have textured surfaces and contoured designs that promote natural movement and weight distribution. This single accessory makes standing comfortable for 8+ hours daily.

Desk Organizer and Cable Management

Standing desks require more thoughtful cable management than traditional setups since cords are more visible when you're elevated. A desk organizer system keeps your workspace tidy and prevents tangled cables. Consider cable trays, clips, and sleeves specifically designed for standing desk setups. Good organization reduces distractions and creates a professional appearance, especially for video calls.

Optional But Beneficial Accessories

A footrest or balance board encourages active standing by allowing subtle movements throughout the day. Wobble cushions, tilting foot rests, and balance boards engage your core muscles while standing. A desk lamp with adjustable positioning ensures proper lighting at your standing height, reducing eye strain. A phone stand at eye level reduces neck strain during calls, and a document holder positions reference materials at the same height as your monitor.

What the Experts Say

Ergonomic specialists and occupational therapists consistently emphasize that accessory selection is just as important as the desk itself. The American Chiropractic Association notes that proper monitor positioning prevents cervical spine strain, while the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) recommends anti-fatigue mats for workers standing more than 4 hours daily. Standing desk experts also stress the importance of an adjustable setup—the best standing desk configuration is one that changes frequently throughout the day, which is why adjustable accessories and proper positioning tools are essential.
