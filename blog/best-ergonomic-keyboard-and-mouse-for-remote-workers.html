Remote workers spend countless hours at their desks, and the keyboard and mouse you choose can make the difference between a productive, pain-free day and one filled with wrist strain, shoulder tension, and frustration. What to Look For

  • Split or Contoured Design: Look for keyboards with a natural wrist position in mind. Split keyboards or those with a gentle slope reduce ulnar deviation (inward wrist bending) that causes strain during all-day typing sessions. Your wrists should remain neutral and straight, not bent at angles.
  • Vertical or Semi-Vertical Mouse: A mouse that positions your hand vertically or at 45 degrees promotes a more natural arm posture than flat, traditional mice. This reduces pronation of the forearm and takes pressure off the shoulder and elbow joints—critical when you're clicking for 8+ hours daily.
  • Adjustable Height and Connectivity Options: Remote workers need flexible setups that adapt to their standing desk or seated workspace. Wireless options with adjustable DPI settings give you control without added desk clutter, while multiple connectivity options ensure compatibility across your work devices.
