Frequently Asked Questions

Is 48 inches wide enough for a home office?

Yes, unless you're running three monitors side-by-side. For one large monitor or two standard monitors with a laptop, 48 inches gives you breathing room without the bulk of 60+ inch commercial desks. Most home offices are 5x7 or 5x8 feet—this width balances functionality with not dominating the room.

Why single motor instead of dual motor?

Single motor means one point of failure instead of two, simpler troubleshooting if something breaks, and lower price. For loads under 220 pounds, it performs identically to dual-motor frames. You only need dual-motor if you're consistently maxing out capacity or want maximum stability with very heavy desks at full height extension.

What desktop should I pair with this frame?

Solid wood (oak, maple, walnut) looks professional but adds weight. Laminate or engineered wood tops are lighter and cheaper ($150-300). Check the frame's weight capacity and subtract your intended accessories, then choose accordingly. Avoid glass tops—they'll show dust constantly and add unnecessary weight for no practical benefit in a home office.

How stable is it at full standing height?

Stability is excellent—almost no wobble when typing normally. If you're hammering on the keyboard aggressively or have an extremely light frame paired with heavy items cantilevered off the edge, you'll notice minor movement. For standard working, it's rock-solid, which the 4.3 rating confirms across real-world use.

Is this better than IKEA or Amazon Basics standing desks?

Yes, but you're paying more. IKEA and budget frames ($200-350) are fine if you'll use it casually, but after 12-18 months, motor issues appear frequently.
