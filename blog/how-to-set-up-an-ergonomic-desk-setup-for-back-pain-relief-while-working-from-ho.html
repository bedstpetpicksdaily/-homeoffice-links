Back pain while working from home has become one of the most common complaints among remote workers, and for good reason—most home offices aren't designed with ergonomics in mind. What to Look For

  • Lumbar support that matches your spine's natural curve: Your lower back has a natural inward curve (lordosis), and your chair needs to support this, not flatten it. Look for chairs with adjustable lumbar support or ones specifically designed for lower back relief, as this is where most work-from-home back pain originates.
  • A desk height that keeps your elbows at 90 degrees: When your arms rest on your desk with your elbows bent at a right angle, your forearms should be parallel to the ground. This prevents shoulder strain and reduces compensatory stress on your lower back—an adjustable or standing desk makes this easier to achieve.
  • Monitor positioning at eye level, about an arm's length away: A monitor that's too low forces you to crane your neck forward, which increases pressure on your lumbar spine. Your eyes should naturally land on the upper third of the screen when you sit up straight with good posture.

What to Avoid

  • Cheap gaming chairs with aggressive lumbar support: While they look impressive, most gaming chairs have fixed, over-aggressive lumbar support that doesn't match most people's actual spine curves. This creates pressure points rather than relief, and you can't adjust them to your specific needs.
  • Kitchen or dining tables as permanent desks: These are typically 30 inches high, which is too high for proper arm positioning and forces your shoulders up toward your ears. This creates neck and shoulder tension that radiates down to your lower back.
  • Placing your monitor on books or boxes for height: While the instinct is right (monitor should be higher), this creates an unstable setup and often leaves it at an awkward angle. Invest in a proper monitor arm or desk riser instead—they're inexpensive and adjustable.
