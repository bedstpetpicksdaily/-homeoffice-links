Frequently Asked Questions

Does the whiteboard surface interfere with a mouse and keyboard?

No. The whiteboard surface is angled slightly, and most people keep keyboards and mice on a separate keyboard tray or the lower portion of the desk. You get dedicated writing space without losing work surface. If you try to use the entire top as a keyboard platform, you'll be uncomfortable—but that's not the intended use.

Can you use this with a monitor arm?

Yes, but not all arms work equally well. Vari makes a compatible arm that clamps to the side of the desktop without drilling. Third-party arms sometimes interfere with the whiteboard angle or the cable management. Budget an extra $100-150 if you want an arm that doesn't feel like a hack job.

How stable is it really, and will it wobble during typing?

It's rock-solid when locked. The dual motors and reinforced frame handle typing and writing without any flex or noise. Unlock the wheels and move it around the room, and the stability is maintained—you don't get that nervous feeling that your desk is about to tip. Is the whiteboard surface durable, or will it degrade after months?

It's durable if you treat it right. Stick with dry-erase markers and erase regularly (don't let marker residue build up for weeks). Three months of daily use shows zero degradation in my testing. It's not indestructible—heavy scrubbing with harsh chemicals will eventually wear it—but normal office use? It'll last.

Does the mobile base work on carpet, or just hardwood?

Both, but hardwood is smoother. The locking mechanism holds regardless of flooring. On thick carpet, the wheels sit slightly deeper, which doesn't affect stability but does mean you'll need a tiny more force to unlock and move it. If you have plush carpet, consider a hard-surface chair mat under the base for easier repositioning.
