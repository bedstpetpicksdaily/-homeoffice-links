What to Look For

  • Full range of motion with smooth adjustments: Your monitor arm should move seamlessly between sitting and standing heights without resistance. Look for gas spring mechanisms rather than friction-based systems—they'll make dozens of daily adjustments feel effortless, not like a chore that discourages you from changing positions.
  • VESA compatibility and weight capacity that exceeds your needs: Check your monitor's VESA mounting pattern (usually 75x75 or 100x100) and verify the arm can handle your monitor's weight plus future upgrades. An arm rated for 10 lbs that holds your 8 lb monitor will eventually sag; get one with 15+ lb capacity for longevity.
  • Desk mounting stability without permanent damage: Clamp-based mounts beat through-desk bolts for renters and those who value flexibility. The clamp should distribute pressure evenly and include protective pads—you want your desk surface intact when you eventually move or upgrade.

What to Avoid

  • Budget friction-based arms: Those $30-50 arms with tension knobs might work initially, but they either stay too loose (monitor drifts down by afternoon) or require two hands and serious effort to adjust (discouraging frequent repositioning). Skip this false economy.
  • Single-monitor arms when you're using dual displays: A single arm that supports two monitors creates leverage problems and weight distribution issues specific to standing desks, where you need precision positioning at multiple heights.
