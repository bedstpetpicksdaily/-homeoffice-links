If you've invested in a standing desk, you're already taking a major step toward better ergonomics and productivity. But here's the thing: without the right keyboard tray, you're likely undoing all that good work by hunching forward or straining your wrists. A quality keyboard tray transforms your standing desk from a back-pain waiting room into a genuinely comfortable workspace where you can actually focus on your work instead of your posture.

What to Look For

  • Adjustable height and tilt: Your keyboard should position your elbows at roughly 90 degrees whether you're sitting or standing, and you need to switch between positions throughout the day. Look for trays that adjust both vertically and offer tilt options so your wrists stay neutral, not bent backward or forward.
  • Smooth gliding mechanism: A keyboard tray is only helpful if you actually use it. Choose one with ball-bearing slides or smooth gliding rails that move effortlessly between sitting and standing heights without resistance or wobbling—this encourages you to adjust it regularly instead of leaving it in one position.
  • Integrated mouse platform: Your mouse should be at the same height and distance as your keyboard to prevent reaching and shoulder strain. Make sure the tray includes a spacious mouse pad section or a separate mousing surface that's just as adjustable as the keyboard portion.

Editor's Tip

Why This Works for This Situation

Standing desks create a unique ergonomic challenge that most keyboard trays don't address: the distance between your body and your keyboard changes dramatically when you switch positions. A fixed keyboard tray works fine when you're always sitting, but at a standing desk, it either sits too high when you're standing (causing shoulder elevation) or too low when you're sitting (forcing you to hunch). The Humanscale system solves this by letting you make micro-adjustments without effort, so you'll actually use it every time you change positions instead of just leaving it wherever it lands.

Beyond ergonomics, the psychological benefit of a smooth, responsive tray shouldn't be underestimated. When your accessories feel cheap or resistant, you unconsciously stop using them and revert to poor habits. A high-quality keyboard tray that glides effortlessly becomes part of your workflow—you'll naturally adjust it, and that consistent micro-movement throughout the day is exactly what standing desks are designed to provide. You get the health benefits of position-switching without the postural compromises that derail most standing desk experiments.

What to Avoid

  • Budget trays with friction slides: Cheap keyboard trays often use friction mechanisms that require significant force to move, making you less likely to adjust throughout the day. You'll end up compromising your posture because it's easier to stay in one position than to fight with a stubborn tray every time you stand or sit.
  • Oversized fixed platforms: Some keyboard trays are massive fixed surfaces that don't adjust independently. These work against standing desks because you can't optimize your positioning for both sitting and standing—you're forced to choose one height that's mediocre for both situations.
  • Mouse-only solutions: A separate mouse platform sounds appealing, but it actually encourages you to reach across your body for your mouse, which defeats the purpose of ergonomic positioning. Your keyboard and mouse need to be integrated on the same adjustable platform.
