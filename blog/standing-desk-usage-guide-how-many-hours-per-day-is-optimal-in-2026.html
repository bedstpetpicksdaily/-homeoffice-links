Frequently Asked Questions

Should I stand for 8 hours a day at a standing desk?

No. Standing 8+ hours daily causes the same fatigue, foot pain, and back strain as sitting all day. Expert research supports 2-4 hours of total standing time spread throughout your workday in 20-30 minute intervals, not continuous standing.

What's the ideal ratio of sitting to standing?

Experts recommend aiming for a 50/30/20 split: 50% sitting, 30% standing, and 20% moving throughout your workday. For an 8-hour day, that's roughly 4 hours sitting, 2.4 hours standing, and 1.6 hours walking or active movement. Start smaller (20-30 minutes standing per hour) and build gradually.

How long does it take my body to adapt to standing desk usage?

Most people build tolerance over several weeks of gradual increases. Start with 1-2 hours of total standing time daily and add 15-20 minutes per week. Jumping to 8+ hours immediately causes fatigue that discourages long-term use. Anti-fatigue mats and footrests significantly speed adaptation by reducing foot strain.

Do I need to buy a $400+ standing desk, or will a cheaper option work?

The desk itself is less critical than the adjustability range (22-48 inches) and ease of adjustment. Electric desks like Sunaofe make frequent transitions effortless, encouraging compliance with recommended intervals. However, anti-fatigue mats, footrests, and a quality sitting option (like COLAMY chairs) often matter more than the desk brand for actual comfort and long-term adherence.

Why do experts recommend anti-fatigue mats if I'm only standing 20-30 minutes at a time?

Even short standing periods cause leg and foot strain without proper support. Anti-fatigue mats absorb impact and encourage micro-movements that prevent fatigue from accumulating. Research shows users with mats are significantly more likely to maintain their standing routine long-term, making the mat investment worthwhile.

Can I use a standing desk without a monitor arm?

Yes, but it's suboptimal. Without a monitor arm that adjusts height, your screen won't be at proper eye level during both sitting and standing, causing neck strain. If you're alternating frequently (as recommended), a monitor arm prevents this discomfort and improves focus during both postures.
