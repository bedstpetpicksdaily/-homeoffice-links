What to Look For

  • Budget-friendly monitor options with VESA compatibility: Look for 24-inch monitors from brands like Dell, LG, or ASUS in the $150-200 range. Ensure they have VESA mounting holes so you can skip the expensive monitor stands and use a dual arm mount instead, which costs less than buying two premium stands.
  • A monitor arm or clamp mount system: A dual monitor arm ($60-120) is one of the smartest investments because it frees up valuable desk space, improves ergonomics, and eliminates the need for two separate monitor stands. This single purchase will actually save you money compared to buying stands separately.
  • An adjustable desk or budget desktop riser: You don't need an expensive electric standing desk right away. A fixed-height manual standing desk ($200-300) or even a desktop riser ($40-80) paired with a quality chair will give you ergonomic flexibility without the premium price tag of motorized options.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What's the cheapest way to set up dual monitors for a home office?

    Buy refurbished or used monitors from Facebook Marketplace, eBay, or local classifieds—you can often find quality 24-inch monitors for $80-150 each. Alternatively, check if your laptop can connect to an external monitor using HDMI or USB-C, which eliminates the need for an expensive docking station.

    Do I need a special graphics card or dock for dual monitors?

    Most modern laptops have at least one video output (HDMI or USB-C), so you don't necessarily need a special card. If your laptop only has USB-C, a basic USB-C hub ($20-40) is often cheaper than a dedicated docking station and works perfectly for two monitors.

    What size monitors should I get for a dual monitor setup on a budget?

    24-inch monitors offer the best balance of cost and usability for remote work, typically ranging from $100-200 refurbished. If you're on an ultra-tight budget, 22-inch monitors are even cheaper and still provide enough screen real estate for productivity apps.

    Can I use an old monitor I already have instead of buying two new ones?

    Absolutely—if you have an old monitor lying around, you can use it as your second display and only buy one new monitor. Just make sure your laptop or computer has enough video outputs (HDMI, DisplayPort, or USB-C) to connect both, or purchase a basic multi-monitor hub if needed.
