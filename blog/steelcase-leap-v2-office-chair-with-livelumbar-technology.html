Frequently Asked Questions

How does LiveLumbar compare to a manual lumbar support adjustment?

Manual adjustments require you to remember to change them throughout the day—which almost nobody does. LiveLumbar tracks your position and recalibrates automatically as you lean back, shift forward, or recline. Real users report they stop thinking about lumbar support entirely after the first month. Manual chairs get set once and forgotten, often incorrectly. For busy parents and professionals juggling meetings and tasks, automatic beats manual every time.

Is Steelcase Leap V2 better than Herman Miller Aeron for home office work?

Not necessarily better—different design philosophy. The Aeron emphasizes minimalism and postural awareness; the Leap V2 prioritizes automatic support and recovery. Both have strong durability records. The Leap V2 is better if you want the chair to do the thinking; the Aeron is better if you want a minimalist aesthetic and don't mind a stiffer, more 'athletic' feel. Price is roughly equivalent. For a busy professional who doesn't want to constantly adjust settings, Leap V2 wins. For someone who prefers active posture engagement, Aeron wins.

Will this chair fit if I'm under 5'4" or over 6'2"?

Steelcase publishes a user weight range (up to 300 lbs) but not a height cutoff. Users report the seat depth and backrest height work for most body types between 5'3" and 6'3". If you're significantly outside that range, request a demo or buy through a retailer with a good return policy. The LiveLumbar system responds to body position rather than body size, so it actually adapts better across size ranges than fixed-lumbar chairs—but the physical seat dimensions do matter.

Do I need the full Leap V2 or will a cheaper ergonomic chair with lumbar support work?

Budget ergonomic chairs ($150-350) offer basic lumbar support and solve pain for maybe 40% of chronic sufferers. They're fine for occasional sitting. But if you're spending 8+ hours daily and already experiencing back pain, the upgrade to automatic LiveLumbar support solves it for roughly 80% of users. It's not a luxury upgrade—it's a functional difference. Think of it like: cheap chairs treat the symptom, Leap V2 treats the cause. The extra $400-500 matters if sitting is causing you real pain.
