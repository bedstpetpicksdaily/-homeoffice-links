What to Look For

  • Adjustable Lumbar Support: Your lower back has a natural curve, and a one-size-fits-all chair won't work. Look for chairs with depth-adjustable or height-adjustable lumbar support that you can customize to your specific spine. This matters because your body is unique, and static lumbar support often sits in the wrong spot after an hour of work.
  • Seat Depth and Width That Fits You: A chair that's too deep will push your lumbar support away from your back, and one that's too shallow won't properly support your thighs. The seat should allow 2-3 inches of space between the edge and the back of your knees when sitting. This prevents circulation issues and keeps you positioned correctly for spinal alignment.
  • Reclining Capability with Locked Positions: Your body needs position changes throughout the day. A good work-from-home chair should recline smoothly (ideally 15-30 degrees) but lock firmly in place once you find your ideal angle. This prevents the annoying slouch that happens when your chair keeps sliding back.
