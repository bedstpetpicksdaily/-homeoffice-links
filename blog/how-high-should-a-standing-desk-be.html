Your standing desk should be set at a height where your elbows are bent at approximately 90 degrees when your arms are relaxed at your sides, with your monitor at or slightly below eye level. For most people, this typically means the desk surface should be between 28 to 48 inches high, depending on your height and proportions.

The Short Answer

The ideal standing desk height allows your elbows to form a 90-degree angle when standing naturally, with your wrists in a neutral position. Your monitor should be positioned at eye level or slightly below (about 15-20 degrees downward). While standard desk heights are around 30 inches, the best height for you depends on your individual measurements—particularly your height and arm length. An adjustable standing desk is the gold standard since it allows you to customize the height for your specific ergonomic needs and switch between sitting and standing throughout the day.

The Full Explanation

Finding the correct standing desk height is crucial for maintaining good posture and preventing strain injuries during extended work sessions. The fundamental principle is ensuring that your upper arms hang naturally at your sides while your forearms are parallel to the ground.

The Elbow Rule: When standing at your desk with your arms relaxed, your elbows should bend at approximately 90 degrees. This positioning ensures that your wrists remain in a neutral, straight position rather than bent upward or downward. Typing or using a mouse with bent wrists can lead to carpal tunnel syndrome and repetitive strain injuries.

Height By Stature: While 30 inches is the standard desk height that works for people around 5'8" to 5'10", taller and shorter individuals need different measurements:

  • People under 5'4": 26-28 inches
  • People 5'4" to 5'8": 28-30 inches
  • People 5'8" to 6'0": 30-32 inches
  • People over 6'0": 32-36 inches or higher

Monitor Positioning: Desk height is only half the equation. Your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when you're looking straight ahead in a neutral posture. If your monitor is too high, you'll lean back and strain your neck. If it's too low, you'll hunch forward and compress your spine. The ideal distance from your eyes to the screen is 20-26 inches.

Keyboard and Mouse Placement: Your keyboard and mouse should sit on the desk surface at the height where your elbows naturally bend at 90 degrees. If you're using an external keyboard and mouse, they should be at the same height as the desk itself. Using an ergonomic keyboard tray positioned slightly below desk height can further reduce strain.

The Dynamic Factor: One of the biggest advantages of a standing desk is the ability to alternate between sitting and standing positions. When you sit down, your desk height should adjust so that your elbows are still at 90 degrees—typically between 28-30 inches. This is why adjustable height desks are so valuable for work-from-home professionals.

What the Experts Say

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) recommends that desk work be performed at a height where the worker's elbows are at approximately 90 degrees. The Ergonomic Society emphasizes that individual variation is significant and that no single height works for everyone. Cornell University's ergonomics research indicates that the most important factor isn't the absolute height, but rather having the flexibility to adjust throughout the day.
