What to Look For

  • Adjustable lumbar support that actually works: Skip chairs with fixed lumbar support. You need a chair where the lumbar curve can be adjusted both vertically and in depth intensity.   • Seat depth and cushioning designed for 8+ hour days: Working from home means long, uninterrupted sitting sessions. Your chair needs a seat that's deep enough to support your thighs without pressing into the back of your knees, with high-density foam that won't compress into an uncomfortable pancake by noon. Look for seats at least 19-20 inches deep and cushioning that maintains its shape after months of use.
  • Reclining capability with proper back angle support: The ability to recline isn't just about comfort—it's biomechanics. A good home office chair should allow you to tilt back 15-20 degrees while maintaining lumbar support, reducing pressure on your spine during focused work sessions. The backrest should move with you, not independently, to keep that critical lower back support engaged.
