What to Look For

  • Lumbar Support System: Your chair needs adjustable or contoured lumbar support that cradles the natural curve of your lower spine. Look for chairs with either adjustable lumbar pillows or built-in lumbar curves that can be positioned to fill the gap between your lower back and the chair. This targeted support is non-negotiable when managing lower back pain—it's the difference between adequate seating and therapeutic seating.
  • Seat Depth and Height Adjustability: An orthopedic chair designed for back pain should allow you to adjust both the seat height and depth independently. Your feet should rest flat on the floor with thighs parallel to the ground, and the seat shouldn't press into the back of your knees. Proper seat dimensions prevent compression of your sciatic nerve and reduce strain on your lumbar spine during extended work sessions.
  • High-Density Foam and Seat Pan Angle: Don't settle for thin cushioning—your chair needs high-density memory foam or gel-infused foam that maintains support without bottoming out. Additionally, look for a seat pan with a slight forward tilt or waterfall edge design that reduces pressure on your thighs and promotes healthy spine alignment throughout your workday.
