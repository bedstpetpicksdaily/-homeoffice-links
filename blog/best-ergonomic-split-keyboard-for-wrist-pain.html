What to Look For When Buying an Ergonomic Split Keyboard

1. Split Angle and Key Separation

Look for keyboards with an adjustable split angle (ideally 15-30 degrees) that allows your hands and wrists to sit in a neutral, relaxed position. The key is that your wrists shouldn't be angled inward or outward—they should be straight. Some keyboards come with fixed angles, which work well if they match your natural hand position, but adjustable versions give you more flexibility as you find your comfort zone.

2. Integrated Wrist Rest Quality

Not all wrist rests are created equal. You want a cushioned rest that's firm enough to provide support but soft enough to reduce pressure points. Memory foam or gel-filled rests are superior to hard plastic. The wrist rest should align with your palm height so your wrist sits flat and neutral, not arched. Check reviews specifically mentioning wrist rest comfort—this is where budget options fail most often.

3. Mechanical Switch Options

Mechanical switches require less force to actuate than membrane keyboards, which reduces finger fatigue and strain on wrists. Look for switches with an actuation force of 45-60g—lighter switches reduce hand strain but shouldn't be so light that you make typos. Cherry MX, Kailh, and Gateron switches are all reliable; the brand matters less than finding the right weight for your preference. Some keyboards let you customize switches, which is a major advantage.

4. Connectivity and Adjustability Features

Wireless options reduce cable clutter and allow more flexible positioning, while USB-C connections are more future-proof. Look for keyboards with programmable keys so you can optimize your layout, and ideally ones that support multiple devices (useful if you switch between laptop and monitor setups). Height and tilt adjustability—even basic feet adjustments—make a real difference in achieving proper ergonomics.
