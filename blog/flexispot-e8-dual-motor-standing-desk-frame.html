Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dual motors deliver genuinely faster height transitions—most users report reaching max height in 10-15 seconds versus 20-30 seconds on single-motor competitors, which matters during an 8-hour workday when you're switching positions frequently
  • ✓ Stability at standing height outperforms most sub-$300 frame alternatives; the dual-motor design distributes weight more evenly, eliminating the wobble that plagues budget frames when you're typing or using a monitor arm
  • ✓ Memory presets actually work reliably—the E8 stores up to 4 positions with millimeter accuracy, so your sitting desk height, standing height, and treadmill desk height all snap back consistently without drift
  • ✓ Motor noise stays reasonable at 50-60dB according to owner reports, comparable to human conversation volume, unlike some budget alternatives that sound like a garage door opening during video calls
Cons
  • ✗ The frame alone costs $400-600 depending on sales, meaning you're paying for motors and mechanics before adding a desktop, which can shock buyers expecting an all-in-one desk solution
  • ✗ Assembly requires either serious patience or a second person—the dual-motor weight distribution means individual components are heavier, and the manual ranges from confusing to frustrating based on the review comments scattered across retailer sites

Our Verdict

The FlexiSpot E8 Dual Motor Frame justifies its price if you spend genuine time sitting and standing throughout the day—the dual-motor speed and stability create noticeably better daily experience compared to single-motor frames priced $200-300 cheaper. However, if you're a casual standing desk user who adjusts position twice daily and budget is tight, a quality single-motor alternative saves you $150-200 without meaningful impact on your workflow. Frequently Asked Questions

How does the E8 compare to the FlexiSpot E7 for the price difference?

The E7 uses a single motor while the E8 dual-motor setup provides 40-50% faster height adjustments and noticeably superior stability, especially if you have a heavier desktop or multiple monitors. The E7 costs roughly $150-200 less, so you're paying premium for speed and wobble elimination. For casual users, the E7 delivers 85% of the experience at 70% of the price.

Will the E8 work with my existing desk, or do I need to buy a new top?

The frame accepts any desktop 48 inches or wider that weighs under 220 pounds—you can reuse your current desk, which is where real savings happen. Many buyers purchase the frame and pair it with a $100-150 butcher block or hardwood top from hardware stores instead of the FlexiSpot branded desktops, cutting total cost by 30-40%.

Is the dual-motor system worth it if I'll barely adjust height during the day?

Honestly, no. If you're adjusting once or twice daily, single-motor frames do the job fine. The dual-motor investment pays off when you're genuinely alternating between sitting and standing multiple times per work session—studies suggest this happens 3-5+ times for people using standing desks to their full potential. If that's not your pattern, save the $200 and invest it elsewhere.
