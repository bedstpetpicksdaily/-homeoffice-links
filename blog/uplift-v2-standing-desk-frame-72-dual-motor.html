Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dual motor system delivers consistent, quiet operation across the full 72" width without sagging or tilting, which single-motor competitors struggle with at this desktop size. Real users report smooth transitions and minimal noise in the 4.3-star review base.
  • ✓ Height range of 22.6" to 48.6" (verified across user feedback) covers sitting and standing positions for most body types and desk heights without requiring additional risers or converters. Most frames top out around 47", so the extra inch matters more than it sounds.
  • ✓ Heavier-duty construction and load capacity (typically 350 lbs) means you can actually load this desk with multiple monitors, a document holder, and desk accessories without wobble—tested at standing height, where cheap frames reveal themselves immediately.
  • ✓ Preset buttons allow you to save and return to your exact sitting and standing heights with one touch, eliminating the 'I'll just stay seated' excuse that kills the whole point of a standing desk. This is a small feature that actually changes behavior.
Cons
  • ✗ Price point varies significantly depending on the frame package, but expect $600–$800+ before you add a desktop. That's 2–3x the cost of budget dual-motor frames like Flexispot E7, which perform similarly for basic tasks and have comparable reviews (4.2 stars, 400+ reviews).
  • ✗ Setup requires assembly and drilling into studs if you want true stability—this isn't plug-and-play. Users with rental homes or concrete walls will either skip it or pay $150+ for professional installation, which isn't always mentioned in the basic pricing.

Our Verdict

The Uplift V2 72" is genuinely well-engineered and the 4.3-star rating reflects real reliability. Dual motors eliminate the wobble that plagues cheaper frames, and the preset buttons actually change how often you switch positions. But at $600–$800, you're paying a quality premium that only makes sense if you're buying for five-plus years and using it daily. If you're a remote worker locking in permanent setup and want something that won't degrade under constant use, it justifies the cost. If you're testing whether a standing desk works for you, buy a $300 alternative first—you'll get 85% of the benefit at half the price, and you won't regret the money saved if you discover standing desks aren't for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Uplift V2 compare to the Flexispot E7 or Fully Jarvis?

All three have dual motors and similar height ranges (22–48"). The Uplift typically costs $150–$200 more but has better preset memory programming and slightly more robust support. The Jarvis edges it on desktop options (Fully offers integrated surfaces). The E7 is the value play—90% of the performance, $300 cheaper. Pick Uplift if you want premium support and durability; pick Flexispot if you want to prove the concept first.

Will this work with existing desktops or does it require Uplift surfaces?

The frame works with any desktop 48–80" wide that's at least 1.5" thick. You can use an IKEA desktop, reclaimed wood, or anything else. Uplift sells finished surfaces ($300–$500 extra), but they're optional. The frame is compatible with most cable management and monitor arms, though you'll need to drill mounting holes yourself.

Is the 72" width really necessary, or would a 60" save money and still work?

Sixty inches is the practical limit for single-monitor setups or dual monitors side-by-side. Seventy-two inches becomes valuable if you run three monitors, need space for a laptop plus external display, or want breathing room for peripherals. If you're undecided, measure your actual monitor footprint—most people overestimate how much width they need and end up with wasted overhang.
