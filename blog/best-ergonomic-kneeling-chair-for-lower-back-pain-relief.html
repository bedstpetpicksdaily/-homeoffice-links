    Frequently Asked Questions

    Do kneeling chairs actually help lower back pain?

    Yes, kneeling chairs can help lower back pain by promoting an open hip angle (around 110-120 degrees) that naturally aligns your spine and reduces strain on the lumbar region. However, they work best when combined with proper posture and shouldn't be used for more than 20-30 minutes at a time, as prolonged kneeling can strain your knees.

    How long does it take to adjust to a kneeling chair?

    Most remote workers need 1-2 weeks to adjust to a kneeling chair, starting with 15-20 minute sessions and gradually increasing duration. Your knees and thighs may feel pressure initially, but this typically subsides as your body adapts and muscles strengthen.

    What's the difference between a kneeling chair and a regular office chair for back pain?

    Kneeling chairs distribute your body weight between your shins and buttocks, creating a forward-tilted posture that keeps your spine naturally aligned and reduces compression on lower back discs. Traditional office chairs encourage slouching and place more pressure directly on your lumbar spine, which can worsen back pain over time.

    Can I use a kneeling chair all day if I have lower back pain?

    No—kneeling chairs are designed for intermittent use (20-30 minutes per session) and should be alternated with a proper ergonomic office chair or standing desk throughout your workday. Using one exclusively can lead to knee strain and may eventually worsen back pain due to repetitive pressure on the same joints.
