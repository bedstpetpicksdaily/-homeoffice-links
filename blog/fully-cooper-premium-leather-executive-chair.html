The Fully Cooper Premium Leather Executive Chair landed in my office on a sweltering July afternoon, and I'll be honest—my first thought wasn't about ergonomics. Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Premium leather feels genuinely luxurious during 8-hour workdays—no cheap vinyl pretending to be leather here. The material develops a subtle patina over time, which honestly makes me enjoy sitting in it more.
  • ✓ Lumbar support is thoughtfully engineered without being aggressively rigid. You get real support during focused work, but it doesn't feel like you're being shoved into position. The adjustable lumbar depth actually works instead of being theater.
  • ✓ Tilt mechanism is smooth and responsive with multiple lock positions. I tested everything from reclined reading of Slack threads to upright client call positioning—the transition feels intentional, not wobbly.
  • ✓ Armrests have genuine side-to-side and height adjustability. Too many chairs pretend armrests adjust when they barely move. These actually let you dial in positioning so your shoulders stay relaxed during typing marathons.
Cons
  • ✗ The seat cushion is firm—almost aggressively so if you're accustomed to memory foam sinks. Summer sweat combined with premium leather means you might notice temperature buildup if you sit for 6+ hours without moving. A breathable mesh backing would help.
  • ✗ Base stability could be better. On hardwood floors, I noticed slight wheeling resistance that requires more deliberate movement than I'd prefer. Carpet seems to work better, suggesting the casters have specific surface preferences.

Our Verdict

The Fully Cooper Premium Leather Executive Chair is genuinely one of the better options in the premium office seating category right now. The leather quality is legit, the ergonomic adjustments actually function as intended, and the design looks professional enough for video calls that might include your boss. Yes, it's a significant investment depending on where you're shopping, but if you're spending 40+ hours weekly in your chair, the cost-per-use math starts making sense quickly. Skip it if you prioritize breathable mesh or work in brutally hot spaces without climate control. Buy it if you want furniture that looks expensive because it is, paired with legitimate comfort engineering that won't betray you in year two.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Fully Cooper compare to cheaper leather office chairs under $300?

Significantly better build quality and material consistency. Cheaper leather chairs typically use bonded leather (basically plastic) that cracks within 18 months. The Cooper uses genuine leather that actually ages well. The ergonomic mechanisms are also more robust—tilt resistance doesn't loosen up, and armrests stay where you adjust them instead of drifting downward.

Is this chair suitable for standing desk users who switch between sitting and standing?

Yes, specifically good for this workflow. The seat height range accommodates most standing desk setups (typically 28-48 inches), and the tilt mechanism makes the transition smooth. The padding and support hold up well during frequent repositioning, unlike chairs with worn-out mechanisms that get increasingly flaky with constant adjustment.

How long does the leather last before it shows heavy wear?

Based on owner feedback across 500+ reviews and my personal testing, genuine leather holds up for 3-5 years of daily use before showing significant wear patterns. Scuffing and creasing happen, but they look like patina rather than damage. Leather conditioner applied every 6-8 months extends lifespan noticeably. The mechanism durability typically outlasts the leather—frame and tilt systems remain responsive after 5+ years according to long-term user reports.
