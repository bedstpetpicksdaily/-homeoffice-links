How High Should a Standing Desk Be for Your Height

Your standing desk height should position your elbows at a 90-degree angle when your arms rest naturally at your sides, with your screen at eye level. As a general rule, the desk surface should be approximately 28-30 inches high for average-height adults, but your ideal height depends on your specific measurements.

The Short Answer

The correct standing desk height is determined by your elbow height when standing naturally. Your desk should align with your elbows when your arms hang at your sides with a 90-degree bend. For most people between 5'8" and 6'0", a desk height of 28-30 inches works well, but taller or shorter individuals may need adjustments of 1-3 inches in either direction. The key is maintaining proper ergonomic posture to prevent strain and injury during long work sessions.

The Full Explanation

Finding your ideal standing desk height requires understanding the relationship between your body measurements and proper ergonomic positioning. Here's what you need to know:

Calculate Your Perfect Height

The most accurate method is to measure your own elbow height. Stand barefoot with your arms relaxed at your sides and your elbows bent at 90 degrees. Have someone measure from the floor to your elbow crease—this is your ideal desk height. Alternatively, you can use a formula: take your height in inches, multiply by 0.45, and round to the nearest half inch.

Height-Based Guidelines

As a starting point, here are general desk heights by person height:

  • Under 5'2": 24-26 inches
  • 5'2" to 5'6": 26-28 inches
  • 5'6" to 5'10": 28-30 inches
  • 5'10" to 6'2": 30-32 inches
  • Over 6'2": 32-34 inches

These are starting points, not absolute rules. Individual proportions vary—someone with longer legs and a shorter torso will need different measurements than someone with different proportions, even at the same total height.

Screen Positioning Matters Equally

Your desk height is only half the equation. Your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when you're looking straight ahead. The screen should be 20-26 inches away from your eyes. If your monitor is too low, you'll crane your neck downward; too high, and you'll experience upper back strain. Proper desk height combined with correct monitor positioning is essential for true ergonomic comfort.

The Keyboard and Mouse Factor

When your hands rest on the keyboard with your elbows at 90 degrees, your wrists should be in a neutral position—not bent up or down. Your forearms should be roughly parallel to the ground. This positioning reduces carpal tunnel strain and repetitive stress injuries. If your desk is too high, you'll have to reach up with your arms, causing shoulder tension. Too low, and you'll bend your wrists downward.

What the Experts Say

Ergonomics professionals from organizations like the American Chiropractic Association and OSHA recommend the 90-degree elbow method as the gold standard for desk height determination. Research published in ergonomics journals shows that proper desk height significantly reduces complaints of neck, shoulder, and lower back pain among standing desk users.
