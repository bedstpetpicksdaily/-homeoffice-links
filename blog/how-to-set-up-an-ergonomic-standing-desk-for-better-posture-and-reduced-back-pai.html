What to Look For

  • Adjustable Height Range (28-48 inches): Your desk needs to transition smoothly between sitting and standing positions. Look for electric models with memory presets so you can save your ideal sitting height (elbows at 90 degrees) and standing height (elbows at 90 degrees, with a slight forward lean).   • Stability and Surface Area: A wobbly desk undermines ergonomics and creates tension as your body compensates for instability. Choose desks with dual-motor systems or robust single motors, and ensure the work surface is at least 48 inches wide to accommodate your monitor, keyboard, and documents without crowding. Crowding forces awkward reaches that strain your shoulders and neck.
  • Cable Management and Monitor Compatibility: Your setup is only ergonomic if your monitor sits at eye level (top of screen at or slightly below eye line, about 20-26 inches away). Look for desks with built-in cable trays and enough clearance underneath for monitor arms or adjustable stands. Poor monitor positioning negates all other ergonomic benefits.
