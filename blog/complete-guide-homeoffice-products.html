Frequently Asked Questions

What office chair should I buy for working from home all day?

Look for an ergonomic chair with lumbar support, adjustable seat height, and armrests to prevent back pain during long work sessions. Popular options include Herman Miller Aeron, Steelcase Leap, or budget-friendly alternatives like IKEA Markus or Amazon Basics ergonomic chairs that cost under $300. Ensure the chair has a tilt mechanism and breathable mesh to keep you comfortable for 8+ hours daily.

Do I need a standing desk or can a regular desk work for remote work?

A regular desk works fine, but adjustable standing desks reduce back strain and improve circulation by allowing you to alternate between sitting and standing throughout the day. If budget is tight, a basic 48-inch desk ($150-300) is sufficient, but electric standing desks ($400-800) offer health benefits worth considering for long-term remote work. You can also use a desk converter on your existing desk as a cheaper alternative.

What lighting do I need for a home office to reduce eye strain?

Use a combination of ambient lighting and a dedicated desk lamp with at least 500 lux brightness positioned to the side of your monitor to eliminate glare and shadows. LED desk lamps ($30-100) with adjustable color temperature (3000K-4000K) are ideal, and position your monitor 20-26 inches away at eye level to further reduce eye fatigue. Avoid placing lights directly behind your screen as this creates contrast and strain.

What accessories do I really need for a productive home office?

Essential items include an external monitor (improves posture vs. laptop screens), wireless keyboard and mouse (reduces strain), and a webcam if your laptop camera is poor quality for video calls. Add a desk organizer, monitor stand, and laptop riser to optimize your setup, plus noise-canceling headphones if you have background noise at home. Most remote workers also benefit from a desk pad, phone stand, and cable management system to stay organized and focused.
