Frequently Asked Questions

What ergonomic chair features should I look for to prevent back pain while working from home?

Look for chairs with adjustable lumbar support, seat height adjustment, and armrests to maintain proper spinal alignment during 8+ hour workdays. A chair with a breathable mesh back and recline function (around 120 degrees) will reduce strain and increase comfort throughout the day.

Do I need a standing desk or can a regular desk work for remote work?

While a regular desk can work, standing desks or adjustable height desks are recommended because they allow you to alternate between sitting and standing, reducing sedentary strain and improving circulation. If budget is tight, a desk riser or converter is a cheaper alternative to achieve the same benefits.

What lighting setup is best to reduce eye strain during video calls and computer work?

Use indirect ambient lighting with a color temperature of 4000-5000K to match daylight, combined with a task light positioned to the side of your monitor to prevent glare. Avoid placing lights directly behind your screen during video calls, as this makes you appear backlit and strains your eyes from the brightness contrast.

Is a webcam, microphone, and ring light necessary or can I use my laptop's built-in camera?

Built-in laptop cameras often produce grainy video and poor audio quality that hurt your professional image on calls. A budget-friendly external webcam ($50-80), USB microphone ($30-60), and basic ring light ($20-40) significantly improve video call quality and are worth the investment if you have frequent client or team meetings.
