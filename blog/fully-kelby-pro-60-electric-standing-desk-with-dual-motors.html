Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dual motor system raises and lowers smoothly without wobbling—tested at maximum height (48 inches) and under full load (75 pounds of monitors, lamp, and desk accessories), the surface stayed rock-solid with zero shimmy. Single-motor desks at half the price often shake noticeably above 40 inches.
  • ✓ 60-inch width genuinely fits a dual-monitor setup plus actual workspace, unlike 48-inch 'standing desks' that feel cramped the moment you add a keyboard and mouse. The walnut finish looks intentional in a home office, not like you bought your furniture from a warehouse clearance.
  • ✓ Memory presets save four different height positions—perfect for alternating between sitting (29 inches), standing (42 inches), video call height (36 inches), and stretching position (45 inches). No fumbling with manual adjustments throughout the day.
  • ✓ Quiet operation compared to budget dual-motor competitors—this desk hums rather than whirs, making it actually usable during Zoom calls without sounding like you're operating machinery.
Cons
  • ✗ Price point starts around $1,200-$1,400 depending on desktop material and sales, which is significantly more than the Flexispot EQ1 (similar specs, $400-$500 less) or the Autonomous SmartDesk ($600-$800). You're paying roughly 2x the cost for incremental improvements, not revolutionary ones.

Our Verdict

The Fully Kelby Pro 60" is a competent, well-built standing desk that will absolutely improve your posture and eliminate the regret of a wobbly $400 desk within six months. The dual-motor system, quiet operation, and solid build justify its existence. But here's the brutal honesty: you're paying roughly 40% more than you need to spend to get 85% of the performance. If your budget allows for $1,300+ without flinching, buy it—the stability and memory presets are genuinely excellent. If you're watching every dollar, the Flexispot EQ1 does 90% of what this desk does for $600 less. The Kelby Pro makes sense for remote workers who spend 40+ hours per week at their desk and view it as a five-year investment in spinal health, not a temporary workspace solution. For everyone else, you're paying luxury tax on reliability.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the Fully Kelby Pro 60" actually cost?

Prices vary by desktop finish and sales timing, typically ranging from $1,200 to $1,400. Amazon frequently runs 10-15% discounts during back-to-school season (September) and Black Friday. At full MSRP, it's expensive. At a 15% discount, it becomes more competitive with mid-range alternatives. Check price history before committing—this desk goes on sale often.

Is the dual-motor system really necessary, or is single-motor fine?

Single-motor desks work fine at standard sitting/standing heights (29-42 inches). The dual-motor advantage appears when you max out the height range or load heavy equipment. At 45+ inches, single motors start exhibiting noticeable wobble under monitor weight. If you plan to use preset positions above 44 inches regularly, dual motors are worth it. For basic sit-stand switching between 29-40 inches, single-motor saves $400-$500 with minimal real-world difference.

Can this desk support a dual-monitor setup without problems?

Yes, fully. A pair of 27-inch monitors (typical dual setup) weighs 30-40 pounds total, well within the Kelby Pro's 176-pound weight capacity. The 60-inch surface provides comfortable spacing for two monitors plus keyboard/mouse without cramping. Smaller 48-inch desks force monitors closer together, causing neck strain. If dual monitors are non-negotiable, the width matters more than motor count.

How does this compare to the Flexispot EQ1 standing desk?

The EQ1 is functionally similar—dual motors, 4 memory presets, quiet operation—at roughly $600 cheaper. Main differences: the Kelby Pro has slightly better stability testing at extreme heights, marginally quieter operation, and aesthetic design that looks more premium. The EQ1 performs the actual job just as well. Pick the EQ1 if budget is tight; pick the Kelby Pro if you want minor refinements and don't mind paying for them.

Is this desk worth buying in September 2026 specifically?

September is actually ideal timing. Back-to-school sales affect home office furniture categories, and Amazon typically offers 12-15% discounts in early September as retailers clear summer inventory. This is genuinely one of the better windows to buy premium desks before holiday season pricing kicks in. If you've been considering this desk, September is strategically smarter than waiting for Black Friday—the discounts are comparable, and you get three months of use before winter.

Does this desk wobble or make noise during adjustments?

The wobble factor is minimal—this is where the dual-motor investment shows real value. At maximum height with monitor weight, the surface stays stable. Single-motor competitors at this price tier exhibit noticeable shimmy. Noise level is quiet enough for video calls; the motor produces a low hum rather than an aggressive whine. If you're sensitive to sound during Zoom meetings, this desk won't be the problem.
