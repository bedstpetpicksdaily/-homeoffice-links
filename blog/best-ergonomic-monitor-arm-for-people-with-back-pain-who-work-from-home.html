    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can an ergonomic monitor arm really help with back pain?

    Yes, a quality monitor arm reduces back pain by positioning your screen at eye level, eliminating the need to hunch forward or crane your neck. Proper screen positioning reduces strain on your cervical and lumbar spine, especially during 8+ hour workdays. Combined with proper chair support and desk setup, monitor arms are one of the most effective ergonomic improvements remote workers can make.

    What height should my monitor arm be positioned to avoid back pain?

    Your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when sitting upright with your shoulders relaxed. The screen should be 20-28 inches away from your face, and the arm should allow full adjustment range to accommodate different chair heights and desk depths. Most ergonomic monitor arms offer both vertical and horizontal adjustment to find your optimal position.

    Is a single monitor arm or dual monitor arm better for back pain?

    A single monitor arm is often better for back pain prevention because it encourages you to position one screen directly in front of you at eye level. Dual monitor arms can work, but they may tempt you to angle monitors awkwardly or position them too wide apart, causing you to rotate your spine and neck. If you need two monitors, consider a single-arm setup with one monitor, or ensure both monitors are directly centered and angled toward your face.

    What monitor arm features should I look for if I have chronic back pain?

    Look for arms with smooth, easy gas spring adjustment (no tools needed), sturdy weight capacity that handles your monitor without drooping, and full range of motion including height, tilt, and rotation. Quick-adjust features are important because you'll want to make micro-adjustments throughout the day as your posture shifts. Weighted bases or desk clamp options provide stability, preventing the monitor from shifting during typing, which can cause unconscious posture changes.
