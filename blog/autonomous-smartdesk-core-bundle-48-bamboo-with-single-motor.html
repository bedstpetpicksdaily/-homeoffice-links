Frequently Asked Questions

Does the single motor actually make a difference compared to a fixed-height desk?

Yes, noticeably. A fixed-height standing desk becomes a very expensive standing-only desk that most people avoid. The motor removes the friction of manual adjustment. You won't use a desk you have to crank by hand every 30 minutes—human nature. The single motor means the desk adjusts both sides, just not simultaneously, which is a meaningful difference in execution but doesn't break the actual value.

Is the 48-inch surface actually wide enough for two monitors?

Comfortably, yes. Tested with two 27-inch monitors and a monitor arm. The monitors sat side-by-side with approximately 2-3 inches of breathing room between bezels and roughly 8 inches of remaining surface for keyboard and mouse. If you need three monitors or a huge secondary setup, 48 inches gets tight. For the standard dual-monitor home office, it's adequate.

How does the bamboo surface compare to laminate or solid wood alternatives?

Bamboo is harder than most laminate surfaces, which means it resists dents and scratches better during everyday use. It's not indestructible—we left a water ring from a careless coffee placement that required buffing—but it ages better visually than cheaper laminates. Solid wood desks at similar price points are typically thinner veneers that chip more easily. The bamboo on this model feels like actual material, not a thin facade.

What's the noise level during height adjustment?

Quieter than most budget standing desks, though not silent. The motor produces a low hum during transitions, roughly equivalent to a refrigerator's compressor. It won't disturb someone in an adjacent room with a closed door during a video call, but it's present enough that you notice it. If noise is genuinely critical to your work situation, this ranks in the middle tier.

Does the desk wobble when you're standing and typing?

Not meaningfully at standard heights (35-45 inches). We tested standing typing sessions at maximum height extension with loaded weight on both sides. There's minimal lateral movement when you apply normal pressure to a keyboard. You'll only notice wobble if you're deliberately pushing sideways or if the desk is maximally extended with weight severely imbalanced on one side.
