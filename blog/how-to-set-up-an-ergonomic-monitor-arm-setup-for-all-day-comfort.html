What to Look For

  • Full Range of Motion (Height, Tilt, Rotation, Swivel) — Your monitor arm needs to adjust in all directions. You should be able to position your screen at eye level when seated, then quickly lower it for standing desk work. Look for arms with smooth, tool-free adjustments and at least 4-6 inches of vertical travel. Cheap arms that only tilt won't accommodate your natural movements throughout the day.
  • Weight Capacity and Cable Management — Monitor arms should handle your screen with zero sagging or drift. Check that the arm supports your specific monitor weight (most modern displays are 5-15 lbs). Quality arms include integrated cable routing channels that keep USB, power, and video cables organized and out of your way—this prevents accidental unplugging and reduces desk clutter.
  • Smooth Counterbalance Technology — Gas spring or spring-loaded mechanisms should require minimal effort to adjust. An arm that's too stiff creates friction and frustration; one that's too loose causes your monitor to slowly drift downward. Test the resistance before buying—your arm should hold position with just a light touch to move it, not require two hands to adjust.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What height should my monitor be when using an ergonomic arm?

    Your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when sitting with your back against the chair, about 20-26 inches from your eyes. This prevents neck strain and reduces downward gaze, which is the primary cause of monitor arm-related discomfort for remote workers.

    Do I need a monitor arm with a gas spring or spring loaded?

    Gas spring arms are generally better for all-day comfort because they provide smooth, effortless adjustments and maintain consistent tension as you move the monitor throughout the day. Spring-loaded arms require more force to adjust and can lose tension over time, making them less ideal for frequent repositioning.

    How much monitor weight can a standard ergonomic arm support?

    Most quality ergonomic monitor arms support 17-35 pounds, which covers single monitors up to 34 inches or dual smaller monitors. Check your monitor's weight before purchasing—most modern 24-27 inch displays weigh 8-12 pounds and will work with any standard arm.

    What's the best monitor arm desk mount type for ergonomics?

    Clamp mounts are ideal for ergonomics because they keep your desk surface clear and allow full adjustment range, while grommet mounts require a desk hole. For all-day comfort, choose a full-motion arm (not a fixed or tilting-only arm) so you can freely adjust height, depth, and angle throughout the workday.
