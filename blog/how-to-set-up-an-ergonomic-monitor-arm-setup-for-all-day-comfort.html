What to Look For

  • Full Range of Motion (Height, Tilt, Rotation, Swivel) — Your monitor arm needs to adjust in all directions. You should be able to position your screen at eye level when seated, then quickly lower it for standing desk work. Look for arms with smooth, tool-free adjustments and at least 4-6 inches of vertical travel. Cheap arms that only tilt won't accommodate your natural movements throughout the day.
  • Weight Capacity and Cable Management — Monitor arms should handle your screen with zero sagging or drift. Check that the arm supports your specific monitor weight (most modern displays are 5-15 lbs). Quality arms include integrated cable routing channels that keep USB, power, and video cables organized and out of your way—this prevents accidental unplugging and reduces desk clutter.
  • Smooth Counterbalance Technology — Gas spring or spring-loaded mechanisms should require minimal effort to adjust. An arm that's too stiff creates friction and frustration; one that's too loose causes your monitor to slowly drift downward. Test the resistance before buying—your arm should hold position with just a light touch to move it, not require two hands to adjust.
