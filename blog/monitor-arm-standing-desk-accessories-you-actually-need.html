What to Look For

  • Cable management compatibility: Look for accessories that actually route your cables cleanly without creating tangled messes as your arm moves. Your monitor arm's cable tray or clips should match your specific arm model, and you'll want to consider whether you need additional sleeves or guides for power cords, USB cables, and video connections.
  • Arm weight capacity alignment: Your accessories need to account for the total load on your monitor arm. A heavy monitor paired with a webcam, light ring, and microphone can quickly exceed your arm's rated capacity. Check your arm's specs and add up everything you're mounting—accessories that reduce weight or distribute it better are genuinely worthwhile.
  • Smooth adjustment across your height range: Since you're using a standing desk, your monitor position will change as you sit and stand. Choose accessories that don't interfere with your monitor arm's full range of motion, and prioritize mounts that allow one-handed adjustment without throwing off your entire setup's balance.

Why This Works for This Situation

Standing desk users need monitor positioning that requires almost zero physical effort to adjust. When you're moving from sitting to standing, fiddling with a stiff or poorly balanced monitor arm becomes a barrier to actually using your standing desk. A quality arm with integrated accessories means your cables don't wrap around your neck as you raise and lower your desk, your monitor stays exactly where you positioned it, and you're not spending mental energy fighting with your equipment throughout the day.

What to Avoid

  • Cheap universal clamps for accessories: Don't use generic webcam or ring light clamps on your monitor arm. They typically add unexpected weight at awkward angles and can cause your arm to drift downward throughout the day. Manufacturer-specific mounts cost more but actually stay put.
  • Oversized cable management sleeves: A sleeve that's too thick will interfere with your arm's range of motion, especially as you transition between sitting and standing positions. Measure your cable bundle and choose sleeves that fit snugly rather than ones that "should be fine" with extra space.
  • Accessories that require rewiring: Avoid any setup that demands you reroute cables differently for sitting versus standing positions. Your accessories should work across your entire height range without modification—if they don't, you'll eventually just stop using your standing desk rather than manage the hassle.
