If you're spending hours at a standing desk each day, your keyboard and mouse positioning can make or break your productivity and comfort. A poorly placed keyboard forces your wrists into unnatural angles, leading to fatigue, pain, and lost focus on your actual work. What to Look For

  • Adjustable height and tilt: Your keyboard tray should move independently of your desk surface, allowing you to position it at elbow height whether you're sitting or standing. Look for models with multiple tilt angles (both positive and negative) so you can find your perfect wrist alignment—this prevents repetitive strain injuries that sneak up on remote workers.
  • Smooth gliding mechanism with secure locking: A keyboard tray that wobbles or drifts downward throughout the day destroys your workflow. Choose accessories with ball-bearing slides or friction-lock systems that hold firm once adjusted, so you're not constantly readjusting while you work.
  • Integrated mouse platform or adjacent space: Your mouse should live at the same height as your keyboard—not off to the side forcing shoulder rotation. The best trays include a built-in mouse pad or enough real estate to position both input devices at identical heights, keeping your arms in a neutral, sustainable position.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do keyboard trays work with standing desks?

Yes, keyboard trays are specifically designed to work with standing desks and are essential for maintaining proper ergonomics at variable heights. They mount underneath your desk surface or to the desk frame, allowing you to keep your keyboard and mouse at elbow height regardless of whether you're sitting or standing, which reduces strain on your wrists and shoulders.

What height should a keyboard tray be when standing?

When standing, your keyboard tray should be positioned so your elbows bend at roughly 90 degrees and your wrists remain neutral and straight. This typically means the keyboard should be 28-30 inches from the floor for average-height adults, though adjustable trays allow you to customize this based on your exact desk height and arm length.

How much does a good keyboard tray for standing desk cost?

Quality keyboard trays for standing desks range from $50-$200 depending on features like adjustability, material, and mouse integration. Basic fixed trays start around $30-$50, while premium models with full tilt/swivel adjustment, memory foam wrist rests, and cable management can cost $150-$300.

Can you use a regular keyboard tray on a standing desk?

While you can technically use a regular keyboard tray on a standing desk, it's not ideal because standard trays often aren't designed for the range of heights standing desks accommodate. Purpose-built standing desk keyboard trays feature better adjustability, durability, and ergonomic support specifically engineered for the standing position and frequent height changes.
