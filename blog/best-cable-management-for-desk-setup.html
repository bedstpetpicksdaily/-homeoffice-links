Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cable management system for a standing desk?

Cable trays mounted underneath the desk surface or along the legs work best for standing desks since they move with your desk and keep cables organized and out of sight. Look for aluminum or steel trays with adjustable brackets that can accommodate multiple cables and allow easy access when you need to add or remove connections. Velcro cable ties are essential to use with trays so you can quickly adjust cables as your desk height changes.

How many cables can I hide behind a cable sleeve?

Most cable sleeves can handle 6-15 cables depending on their diameter and the sleeve's capacity rating, which is usually listed as a maximum cable count on the product. For a typical desk setup with monitor, keyboard, mouse, desk lamp, and phone charger, a 1-inch diameter sleeve is usually sufficient. If you have more peripherals like speakers, external hard drives, or a dock, consider a larger 1.5-2 inch sleeve or use multiple sleeves.

What's the cheapest way to organize desk cables?

Velcro cable ties (reusable straps) are the most budget-friendly solution at $5-10 for a pack that lasts years, paired with simple adhesive cable clips for $8-15. You can also use rubber bands, zip ties, or even repurposed items like toilet paper tubes as cable organizers. For under $20 total, you can effectively organize most home office setups without expensive trays or sleeves.

How do I keep cables from falling behind my desk?

Use adhesive cable clips mounted along the back edge or underside of your desk to guide cables down in organized bundles—they cost $10-20 for a set. Pair this with a cable sleeve or spiral wrap to keep cables grouped together, then route them into a cable box or tray at the base of your desk. This prevents cables from getting tangled in chair wheels and makes it easy to quickly unplug and replug devices.
