Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The dual-motor system is genuinely smooth—I tested height transitions from sitting (29") to standing (47") without any wobble or grinding noise that plagues cheaper models. The motors sync perfectly, meaning the desktop stays level throughout the movement. That's not a small detail when you're moving between positions 10+ times daily.
  • ✓ The 60" width is legitimately wide. I fit a 34" ultrawide monitor, keyboard tray, and still had room for a coffee mug and notepad without feeling cramped. If you work with spreadsheets, design software, or need multiple monitor setups, this width actually justifies the price instead of being wasted space.
  • ✓ The grommet cable management system actually works without making you want to scream. Most desks promise cable organization and deliver chaos—this one has a rubberized grommet that guides cables cleanly through the desktop. I ran seven cables through it (monitor, keyboard, mouse, charging cables, headphones) and it looks organized rather than like a tech snake pit.
  • ✓ Memory presets save three custom height positions, which sounds basic until you realize you're not constantly adjusting between your ideal sitting and standing heights. I set 30" for sitting work, 42" for standing while on calls, and 45" for standing while typing. Hitting those buttons eliminates the micro-decision fatigue from manual adjustments.
Cons
  • ✗ The price point varies significantly depending on sales, but this desk consistently lands above $600-700 for the full setup. That's not unreasonable for the quality, but it's a genuine barrier if you're setting up a home office on a budget. July sales sometimes drop it lower, but don't count on it.
  • ✗ The desktop surface itself is particle board, not solid wood or bamboo. It's sturdy enough and handles daily use fine, but it feels cheap compared to the robust metal frame underneath. If you spill anything, the edges aren't sealed particularly well, which is a legitimate concern in a home office where liquids and accidents happen.

Our Verdict

The Fully Jarvis Pro Series 60" deserves its strong rating because it does the core job well—smooth height adjustment, stable construction, and enough width to actually work with. At $600-700, you're paying a legitimate premium over no-name electric desks, but you're getting reliability that won't have you cursing at your workspace six months in. This desk earns its place in the rotation of standing desks that actually work, though it's not revolutionary. Buy it if you spend 40+ hours per week at your desk and can justify the investment. Skip it if you're testing the standing desk concept for the first time—start with something cheaper to see if you'll actually use the height adjustment feature.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Jarvis Pro wobble when standing at full height?

No noticeable wobble at 47". I tested stability by pressing down on the desktop edge while at maximum standing height—the surface remained solid. The dual-motor system keeps the desktop perfectly level, which is critical for monitor placement and not creating a disorienting work environment. Some single-motor desks rock slightly at max height; this one doesn't.

Is the 60" width actually useful or just extra desk space?

Useful, not wasteful. A 60" desktop fits a 34" ultrawide monitor comfortably with workspace on both sides, or two 27" monitors side-by-side with room to spare. If you're only using a single small monitor and keyboard, you don't need 60"—a 48" would be fine. But if you work with design software, coding across multiple windows, or video editing, the width prevents that constant shuffling and window-switching that kills productivity.

How long do the motors typically last?

Fully backs the motors with a seven-year warranty, which is legitimately strong compared to three-year warranties on cheaper competitors. I haven't hit any motor failures in my two-month test, but real-world data from the 500+ reviews shows most users don't report motor issues in their first 2-3 years. The dual-motor design also means if one fails, you can still adjust height with the other motor—you just lose smoothness.
