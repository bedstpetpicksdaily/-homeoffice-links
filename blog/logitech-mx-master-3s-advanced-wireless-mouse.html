Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The scroll wheel customization is genuinely useful. I can switch between click-by-click scrolling and smooth scrolling with a single button press. For someone like me who spends 3 hours daily in spreadsheets and another 2 hours reading documents, this alone saves micro-frustrations. The magnetic scroll wheel feels premium and responsive.
  • ✓ Side-to-side scrolling wheel (the thumb wheel) saves real time when working with multiple monitors or wide documents. I tested this against a standard $30 mouse, and the workflow difference was noticeable after the first week—no more reaching for keyboard shortcuts to pan left-right.
  • ✓ The ergonomic contour is legitimately comfortable for 8+ hour workdays. My hand fatigue dropped measurably compared to my previous Logitech G502 after switching to this. The thumb rest is positioned perfectly if you have medium-to-large hands.
  • ✓ Multi-device connectivity via the Unifying receiver or Bluetooth means I can pair this with my laptop, desktop, and iPad. Switching between devices works seamlessly. I tested it across three screens in my home office setup without a single connection hiccup over 90 days.
Cons
  • ✗ The $99 price point is hard to justify when a Razer Pro Click or even Logitech's own MX Master 2S (still available used) handles 80% of these tasks for $40-60. The 3S improvements are incremental, not revolutionary. If you're on a tight budget building out a home office, this is a luxury item, not a necessity.
  • ✗ The mouse requires USB-C charging, and the cable included is short. If your desk setup doesn't have convenient USB-C access nearby, you'll need an adapter or separate charging solution. Battery lasts 70 days on a full charge, which is excellent—but that first charge setup feels inconvenient compared to AA-battery mice.

Our Verdict

The Logitech MX Master 3S justifies its price if you're a power user handling complex digital workflows (design, data analysis, multi-screen work) and you've already invested in other quality ergonomic furniture. The dual scroll wheels, smooth palm support, and flawless multi-device switching create a genuinely faster experience than budget mice. However, if you're buying your first mouse for a basic home office setup or you're price-sensitive, a $40 ergonomic alternative will serve you 90% as well. At $99, this is a tool for optimizing an already-solid workspace, not a foundation piece. The 4.3-star rating from 500+ users confirms it's reliable—but reliable and worth double the price aren't always the same thing. Spend the extra money only if your daily work genuinely demands it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the MX Master 3S compare to cheaper Logitech ergonomic mice like the MX Vertical?

The MX Vertical ($100) uses a vertical/tilted grip design that reduces forearm rotation—different philosophy entirely. The standard MX Master 3S uses a traditional contoured grip. The MX Vertical is better for people with pronation pain; the Master 3S is better for people who need speed and multi-device switching. Don't confuse them as direct competitors. For pure productivity without specific ergonomic pain, the Master 3S wins. For wrist/forearm strain specifically, the MX Vertical might be the better investment.

Is the USB-C charging a dealbreaker, and how long does the battery actually last?

Not a dealbreaker, but it's an inconvenience compared to AA batteries. I charged mine once every 70 days during heavy daily use (8+ hours). The cable provided is 1.5 meters, which works if your charging port is within arm's reach of your desk. If it isn't, grab a USB-C extension cable for $8-12. The wireless range is strong enough that you can charge it across the room and it'll stay connected. Real-world gripe: the charging LED feedback is subtle, so you might forget it's fully charged.

Does this mouse work with Mac and Windows equally well, or is one OS better supported?

Both work flawlessly. I tested across a 2023 MacBook Pro, a Windows 11 desktop, and an iPad. The cursor speed, button mapping, and responsiveness were identical. Logitech's software (Logi Options+) is available for both systems. One note: if you prefer not to install software, the mouse works out-of-the-box with basic functionality on both systems, but you lose the advanced customization of the scroll wheel. For Mac users specifically, the integration is smooth—no compatibility hesitations.
