Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Gesture compare to Herman Miller Aeron at a similar price point?

Both are excellent, but they're engineered differently. The Aeron is more minimal and better for people who like a firm, straightforward seat. The Gesture has more adjustability and active back support, making it better for people who shift positions frequently or have mild lower back issues. The Gesture also moves with you; the Aeron keeps you more 'locked in.' Test both if possible—preference here is genuinely personal.

Are the natural glides really better than casters, or is that marketing?

They're genuinely better for hardwood and laminate—no catching, no noise, and they won't damage floors over years of use. On carpet, regular casters slightly outperform them because they roll more freely. If you're on any hard surface, the glides justify themselves immediately. You'll appreciate not hearing that metallic squeak at 6 a.m.

Does this chair actually prevent back pain, or will I still need physical therapy?

A better chair helps prevent pain from getting worse and reduces strain during long sitting sessions—it's not a cure. If you already have chronic pain, a chair alone won't fix it. But combined with a desk at the right height, a monitor arm, and occasional movement breaks, the Gesture removes the chair as a pain variable. That's valuable. Talk to a physical therapist about your specific issues; they can confirm whether this chair supports your treatment.
