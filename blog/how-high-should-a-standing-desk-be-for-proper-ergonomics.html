How High Should A Standing Desk Be For Proper Ergonomics

Your standing desk should be positioned so your elbows are at a 90-degree angle when your arms hang naturally at your sides. For most people, this means the desk surface should be between 28-30 inches high, though the ideal height depends on your individual measurements and the type of work you're doing.

The Short Answer

The proper standing desk height allows your elbows to bend at approximately 90 degrees while your arms rest naturally at your sides. Your wrists should be straight and neutral, not bent upward or downward, when your hands rest on the keyboard. Most people find an optimal standing desk height between 28-30 inches, but this can vary based on your height, arm length, and footwear. The key is that your eyes should be level with the top third of your monitor when standing naturally upright.

The Full Explanation

Finding the correct standing desk height is crucial for preventing strain, fatigue, and long-term musculoskeletal issues. Unlike sitting desks, standing desk ergonomics require precise measurements because even small deviations can cause wrist strain, shoulder tension, and back pain over time.

The Basic Formula
To calculate your ideal standing desk height, measure from the floor to your elbow while standing with your arms relaxed at your sides. Your desk surface should be approximately at this elbow height. This ensures your forearms can rest parallel to the ground or at a slight downward angle when typing, which is the most ergonomic position.

Standard Heights by Body Type
While 28-30 inches accommodates most adults, taller individuals (over 6 feet) may need 30-32 inches, while shorter people (under 5'6") might be more comfortable at 26-28 inches. If you share a workspace or have multiple users, an adjustable standing desk that moves between sitting and standing positions is essential.

Monitor Positioning
Height alone isn't enough—your monitor placement matters equally. Position your screen 20-30 inches from your eyes, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. This prevents neck strain and ensures you're not looking down at your work, which is a common cause of cervical spine problems.

Keyboard and Mouse Height
Your keyboard and mouse should be positioned so your wrists remain neutral and straight. Avoid resting your wrists on the desk edge, as this can lead to carpal tunnel syndrome. Your elbows should have about 90 degrees of bend, creating a neutral angle that distributes strain evenly across your arms and shoulders.

The Importance of Footwear
Don't forget that your shoe height affects desk measurements. If you typically wear shoes with 1-inch heels at work, account for this when setting your desk height. Many standing desk users keep comfortable, flat shoes nearby to ensure consistent posture throughout the day.

What the Experts Say

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) recommends that desk surfaces be positioned between 28-30 inches high for standing work. The American Chiropractic Association emphasizes that proper desk height is one of the most critical factors in preventing occupational strain injuries. Ergonomic specialists consistently note that people who invest time in measuring and adjusting their standing desk height experience significantly less fatigue and pain compared to those who guess at the right height.

Dr. James Carter, a leading ergonomics researcher, states: "The 90-degree elbow angle isn't arbitrary—it's based on biomechanics research showing this angle minimizes strain on the rotator cuff, reduces nerve compression, and allows for optimal force distribution during typing and mouse work."
