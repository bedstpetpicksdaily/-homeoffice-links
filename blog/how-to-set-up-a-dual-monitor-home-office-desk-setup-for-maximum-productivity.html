    Frequently Asked Questions

    What size monitors do I need for a dual monitor setup?

    Most productivity experts recommend 24-27 inch monitors as the sweet spot for dual setups, offering enough screen real estate without excessive head turning. If you have a larger desk (60+ inches), you can go up to 32 inches, but anything larger becomes counterproductive since you'll need to move your head too much. Match both monitors to the same size and resolution for consistent scaling and easier window management.

    Do I need a special graphics card or laptop for dual monitors?

    Most modern laptops and desktops support dual monitors, but check your device's video outputs first—you'll need at least two ports (HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C, or Thunderbolt). If your laptop only has one port, you can use a docking station or USB-C hub to add additional display connections without needing a dedicated graphics card upgrade.

    How should I position two monitors on my desk?

    Position both monitors at arm's length away (20-26 inches) with the top of the screens at or slightly below eye level when seated. Place them side-by-side with minimal gap, or use one as primary (directly in front) and angle the second at 30 degrees—whichever matches your workflow. Ensure both screens are tilted slightly upward (15-20 degrees) to reduce neck strain.

    What's the best way to organize windows across two monitors?

    Use your primary monitor (usually the one in front) for your main work application, and dedicate the secondary monitor to communication tools, reference materials, or dashboards. Most operating systems let you snap windows automatically—on Windows use Win+arrow keys, or on Mac use Mission Control and drag windows between screens. This separation reduces context-switching and keeps you focused while maintaining easy access to information you need.
