The Short Answer

The ideal standing desk height is when your elbows bend at approximately 90 degrees with your shoulders relaxed. This typically ranges from 28 to 48 inches from the floor, though your specific height will determine where in this range works best for you. Your wrists should remain neutral and straight, not bent up or down, and your monitor should be positioned at eye level about 20-26 inches away. Getting this measurement right is crucial for preventing back pain, neck strain, and repetitive stress injuries.

The Full Explanation

Understanding proper standing desk height starts with knowing your body's natural posture. When you're standing with good posture, your arms should hang naturally at your sides with elbows bent at 90 degrees. Your desk should align with this elbow height, allowing your forearms to be parallel to the ground when you're typing or using your mouse.

To find your ideal standing desk height, stand upright with your shoes on and measure the distance from the floor to the bend in your elbow. This measurement is your target desk height. However, since desk heights vary and people have different proportions, most adjustable standing desks range from 22 to 48 inches to accommodate users from about 4'10" to 6'6".

Your monitor placement is equally important. The top of your screen should be at or slightly below eye level, and you should be able to view it with your head in a neutral position (not tilted up or down). This prevents neck strain, which is one of the most common complaints from improper standing desk setup.

Your keyboard and mouse should also be at elbow height. If they're too high, you'll experience shoulder and neck tension. If they're too low, you'll slouch forward, defeating the purpose of standing and creating back strain. Proper ergonomic positioning of all these elements together creates a setup that supports healthy posture and reduces fatigue.

What the Experts Say

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) and ergonomic experts consistently recommend the 90-degree elbow angle as the gold standard for desk work. The American Chiropractic Association emphasizes that standing desk height should allow for neutral wrist positioning and relaxed shoulders to prevent cumulative strain injuries.

Dr. Jack Bandy, a certified ergonomics specialist, notes that "proper desk height is the foundation of a healthy standing workspace. Without it, even the best standing desk won't prevent discomfort." Experts also stress that standing desk height is only one piece of the puzzle—you should alternate between sitting and standing throughout the day, take regular movement breaks, and invest in supporting accessories like anti-fatigue mats and document holders.
