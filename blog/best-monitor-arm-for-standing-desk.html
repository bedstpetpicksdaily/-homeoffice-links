What to Look For

1. Gas Spring Quality and Adjustment Range

The heart of any good monitor arm is its gas spring mechanism. Look for arms with smooth, dampened adjustment that won't droop when you let go. Check the weight capacity against your monitor's actual weight (don't just assume—measure it). Premium gas springs hold position reliably for years; cheap ones fail within months. The vertical adjustment range should span at least 20 inches to accommodate both sitting and standing positions.

2. Clamp Type and Desk Compatibility

Clamp-style arms are more versatile than bolt-mounted options, especially for renters or those with non-standard desks. Verify the clamp opens wide enough for your desk thickness (typically 0.4 to 3.15 inches). Check reviews specifically mentioning your desk material—plastic clamps can damage premium wood surfaces, while quality metal clamps work universally. Look for clamps with protective rubber padding.

3. Monitor Compatibility and VESA Standards

Not all monitor arms fit all monitors. Confirm the arm supports your monitor's weight and uses standard VESA mounting (75mm x 75mm or 100mm x 100mm are most common). If you have an ultrawide or curved monitor, verify compatibility explicitly—these require arms with reinforced construction and extended reach. Check the maximum reach distance; it should extend at least 15 inches from your desk edge.

4. Rotation and Tilt Flexibility

The best monitor arms offer full 360-degree rotation, portrait/landscape switching, and tilt adjustments (typically ±30 degrees). This flexibility lets you optimize screen angle for your exact eye level and reduces glare issues. Arms lacking these adjustments lock you into one viewing position, defeating the purpose of investing in an adjustable solution.
