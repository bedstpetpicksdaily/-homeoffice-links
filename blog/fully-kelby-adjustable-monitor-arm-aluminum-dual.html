Frequently Asked Questions

Will this actually improve my posture or is that just marketing?

Yes, but with a caveat. An arm positions your monitors at eye level, which removes the downward head tilt that causes neck strain. However, it's not a posture fix on its own—you still need a decent chair, proper keyboard height, and the discipline to not slouch. Think of it as removing one major obstacle to good posture, not solving all ergonomic problems simultaneously.

How much weight can each arm handle?

The Fully Kelby specs max out around 17-20 lbs per arm depending on configuration. This comfortably handles monitors up to 27 inches; anything larger and you're pushing it. Two 24-inch monitors? Zero problem. Two 32-inch ultrawide displays? You're asking too much and the arm will sag.

Is this better than just buying a monitor stand?

For dual monitors, absolutely. A stand takes up desk real estate and limits adjustability. This arm mounts to your desk edge, freeing up surface area and giving you independent positioning for each screen. If you only have one monitor, a good stand is cheaper. If you have two, this is the smarter investment.

How long does setup actually take if I don't have a power drill?

With a manual drill or screwdriver, probably 25-30 minutes instead of 15. The mounting bracket requires drilling into your desk (usually the desk underside), so it's not a zero-commitment installation. If you're renting, confirm your landlord approves desk modifications first.

Will it work with my laptop as one of the monitors?

Technically yes—there are VESA adapters for laptops—but practically, no. The arm's reach and weight distribution assume you're mounting full monitors. A laptop adds awkward angles and instability. Better to use a separate monitor arm for the laptop or just elevate it with a static stand.
