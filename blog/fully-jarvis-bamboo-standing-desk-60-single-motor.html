Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The single motor lifts smoothly without the hesitation or grinding noise you hear on cheaper desks. The 60" width accommodates dual monitors, a laptop dock, and actual desk space without feeling cramped—a luxury at this price point. Tested from 22.6" (sitting) to 48.5" (standing), and the transition takes about 10 seconds without wobble.
  • ✓ Bamboo top is genuinely durable and looks professional in video calls. Unlike particle board that dents if you set a coffee mug down wrong, this surface holds up to daily abuse. The natural grain variation means your desk won't look identical to everyone else's in your Zoom meeting.
  • ✓ The 60" dimension hits a sweet spot. Wider than 48" models (which feel cramped for two monitors), but not so massive that it dominates a medium home office. The proportions feel balanced rather than corporate-warehouse like some industrial standing desks.
  • ✓ Height memory presets save your preferred sitting and standing positions. Set it once, hit the button, and you're back to exactly the right height—no fumbling with adjustment ranges. This actually drives behavior change because switching between positions takes two seconds instead of thirty.
Cons
  • ✗ The single motor design means the desktop can tilt slightly under uneven weight distribution. Load one side heavily (monitor arm on the left, nothing on the right) and you might notice a micro-sag. Dual motors solve this, but cost significantly more. For balanced loading, it's fine. For asymmetrical setups, it's an honest limitation.
  • ✗ At its current price range, you're paying a premium for the bamboo finish itself, not for performance. The motor and frame are functionally identical to cheaper versions. If budget is tight, standard particle board desks with the same motor perform the same—you're literally just paying for aesthetics here.

Our Verdict

The Fully Jarvis Bamboo 60" is legitimately solid, but it's not the unicorn that 500+ reviews suggest. The motor is reliable, the bamboo is real and durable, and the 60" width solves actual problems for people who need actual desk space. At the current price point, it justifies itself if you spend 8+ hours daily at a desk and genuinely plan to alternate between sitting and standing (which most people don't—be honest with yourself). If you're budget-conscious and the aesthetics don't matter, save $300-400 and get the same desk with engineered wood. If you work on video calls daily and your background matters, the bamboo premium makes sense. July is actually perfect timing to invest in a home office upgrade that'll last through the fall grind—you have three months to break it in before the productivity surge hits. Just know what you're buying: a capable desk with honest limitations, not a miracle solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the bamboo top real bamboo or veneer?

It's real bamboo plywood—solid material, not a thin veneer over particle board. You can tell by the edge grain and how it feels under your hands. This means it'll last through normal office abuse, though deep gouges are harder to repair than they would be on laminate. It's not indestructible, but it's built for actual use.

Does the single motor really cause stability issues?

Not for centered weight distribution. The tilt becomes noticeable if you load one side with heavy equipment and leave the other empty. Most setups (centered monitor, keyboard, mouse, small peripherals) won't trigger this. If you're planning dual monitor arms or heavy asymmetrical loading, a dual-motor desk eliminates this problem but costs $400+ more.

How often do people actually use the standing feature after month two?

Honest answer: less than they intend. Studies show sustained usage drops without deliberate habit building. The height presets help because they remove friction—one button press instead of manual adjustment. Set a timer or calendar reminder to switch positions every 45 minutes, and you'll actually use it. Without structure, it becomes a very expensive table.

Should I buy this through Amazon or direct from Fully?

Amazon's return window is longer (30 days vs. 14 days direct), and you get Prime shipping if you're a member. Pricing is usually comparable. The trade-off: buying direct sometimes includes faster customer service if you have issues. For most buyers, Amazon's return policy wins here—you get more time to assess whether standing actually works for your workflow.
