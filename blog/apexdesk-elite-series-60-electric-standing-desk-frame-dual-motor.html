Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dual motors deliver genuinely smooth, silent operation—I tested raising and lowering with one hand while holding a coffee cup, and zero wobble or strain noise. Single-motor desks in this price range typically hum loudly and drift during partial adjustments; this doesn't.
  • ✓ The 60-inch surface is the sweet spot for dual monitors plus working space without dominating a small bedroom office. I fit a 27-inch ultrawide, laptop stand, and actual desk real estate without feeling cramped, which matters when you're on calls eight hours daily.
  • ✓ Memory presets save three height positions, and I use them religiously—sitting at 28 inches, standing at 42 inches, and a middle ground at 36 inches for sketching. The precision is close enough that recurring positions feel automatic, eliminating the micro-adjustments that waste mental energy.
  • ✓ Build quality suggests this will outlive your job at the company that bought it. Five months in, zero creaking, no loose fasteners, and the cable management clips haven't budged. The frame feels commercial-grade despite the consumer price tag, which is the opposite of most budget standing desks.
Cons
  • ✗ The base footprint is genuinely wide (roughly 28 inches)—my 10x10 home office corner feels tighter than I'd prefer, and if you have a small apartment or shared workspace, measure twice before committing. This isn't a portable desk.
  • ✗ Setup requires a second person or serious patience. The frame arrives in two heavy boxes, assembly takes 90 minutes solo, and the instructions assume you've assembled furniture before. I spent an extra 30 minutes troubleshooting motor alignment because the manual doesn't clarify the left/right orientation clearly.

Our Verdict

The ApexDesk Elite 60" dual motor frame justifies its mid-range investment if you plan to use it daily for three-plus years. The quiet operation, zero wobble, and rock-solid build quality separate it from cheaper alternatives that start degrading after 18 months. At current market pricing, you're paying more than basic single-motor options but significantly less than premium brands charging double—which makes it the practical choice for remote workers who spend 40+ hours per week sitting at their desk. If your budget is tight or you're uncertain about standing desk commitment, rent time on a cheaper model first. If you're serious about ergonomic investment, this frame delivers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quiet is the dual motor really compared to single-motor desks?

I tested it against a single-motor competitor in July, and the difference is noticeable. The dual motors divide the load, so each operates at lower stress—the Elite produces a soft hum for about three seconds during adjustment, while the single-motor desk buzzed aggressively for eight seconds. In open floor plans or quiet offices, this matters. In a closed home office, both are acceptable, but the Elite feels less intrusive.

Will the frame support a 60-pound monitor setup plus desk clutter?

Yes, reliably. The dual motors handle 275-pound capacity, and I intentionally loaded mine heavy—27-inch monitor, laptop stand, three desk organizers, and books. No sag, no drift, no instability. The wobble test (pushing the desktop horizontally) shows minimal flex. That said, if you're mounting three monitors or using the desk for photography equipment, distribute weight toward the center and avoid cantilevering heavy items beyond the front edge.

Is the price difference between dual and single motor worth it?

For most people, yes—but it depends on your usage. If you adjust height five times daily and demand silence during calls, dual motor saves frustration. If you set it to two positions and adjust twice weekly, a single-motor frame works fine. I estimate the dual motor costs 30-40% more but delivers 60% better stability and noise performance, so the math favors upgrading if you can stretch the budget. Think of it as insurance against regret in year two.
