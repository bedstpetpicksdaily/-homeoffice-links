The Full Explanation

Ergonomic best practices consistently recommend that your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when you're seated with your back straight. Your eyes should be roughly 20-26 inches from the screen. Most people place monitors directly on their desk, which positions them too low—forcing your neck into a forward flexion position that creates cumulative stress.

This seemingly minor positioning issue has serious consequences. Over weeks and months, improper monitor height contributes to cervical spine strain, trapezius muscle tightness, and tension headaches. Studies on office workers show that ergonomic interventions—particularly screen positioning—significantly reduce musculoskeletal discomfort.

Monitor arms address this by providing full adjustability: height, tilt, rotation, and depth. Premium models support multiple monitors and accommodate various desk setups. They also free up desk space by moving your monitor off the work surface, creating a cleaner, more organized workspace. For standing desk users, a monitor arm becomes even more valuable, as it allows you to adjust your screen as you transition between sitting and standing throughout the day.

What the Experts Say

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) and ergonomic research organizations consistently recommend monitor positioning as a top priority for desk workers. The American Chiropractic Association notes that screen height and positioning are among the most impactful factors in preventing workplace-related neck pain. Ergonomic specialists universally agree that a properly positioned monitor—whether through a monitor arm or elevated stand—is one of the highest-ROI investments a remote worker can make. Physical therapists treating office workers frequently prescribe monitor arm adjustments as part of pain management and prevention protocols.
