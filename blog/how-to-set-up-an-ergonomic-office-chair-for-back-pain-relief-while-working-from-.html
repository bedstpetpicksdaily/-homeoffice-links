Back pain while working from home has become increasingly common as more people transition to remote work setups. Unlike traditional office environments where ergonomic standards are sometimes enforced, home offices often lack proper furniture designed to support your spine throughout the day. Setting up your chair correctly can be the difference between productivity and persistent pain that affects both your work and quality of life.

What to Look For

  • Lumbar support that matches your spine's natural curve: Your lower back has a natural inward curve (lordosis) that needs proper support. Look for chairs with adjustable lumbar support or built-in lumbar pillows that can be positioned at the small of your back, typically 2-3 inches above your seat. This targeted support prevents the slouching that leads to compression in your lower discs.
  • Seat height and depth that allows 90-degree angles: When seated, your feet should rest flat on the floor with knees at 90 degrees, your hips should be level with or slightly higher than your knees, and your arms should form 90-degree angles at your desk. A chair that doesn't accommodate these measurements will force your body into compensatory positions that strain your back throughout the day.
  • Quality materials and adjustment mechanisms that stay in place: Cheap foam degrades quickly, losing support within months. Look for high-density foam, breathable mesh backs (which reduce heat buildup during long sessions), and sturdy tilt mechanisms that lock in position. Your chair needs to maintain its adjustments throughout an 8-hour workday without creeping or shifting.
