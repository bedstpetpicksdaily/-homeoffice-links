This desk works best for people who've already committed to the standing desk lifestyle and want a desk that won't force them to regret the decision after 18 months. The dual motors, smooth adjustments, and proven durability justify the price for someone who uses their desk 8+ hours daily and has space in their budget. But if you're testing the standing desk waters, recovering from a bad budget desk experience, or counting pennies, the Jarvis Pro is overkill—and honest advice means saying so. Frequently Asked Questions

How fast does it actually move between sitting and standing heights?

The dual motors move at about 1 inch per second, which means a full transition from sitting (28") to standing (46") takes roughly 18 seconds. That's fast enough that you'll actually use it multiple times daily instead of setting it once and forgetting about it. The noise level is genuinely quiet—you won't startle anyone on video calls.

Does it wobble when you're typing at standing height?

Not noticeably, which matters because wobbling desks train you to avoid standing. The Jarvis Pro uses a stabilizing crossbar design that keeps vibration minimal even with vigorous typing or when you're gesturing during video calls. Budget desks in this price range often fail this test, so it's worth noting here.

Is the 60-inch width enough, or should I go bigger?

60 inches fits a dual-monitor setup, keyboard, mouse, and modest workspace without forcing you to mount your second monitor on an expensive arm. If you need more than two monitors or work with large physical materials (design boards, paperwork), consider the 72-inch model. For typical home office work, 60" is the sweet spot between usable space and not dominating your room.

How does the walnut top hold up over time?

Walnut is durable but not indestructible—it scratches and can dent with impact. Real talk: if you're rough on furniture or have kids/pets regularly jumping on your desk, this finish will show wear. That said, walnut darkens beautifully as it ages, so minor dings often blend into the character rather than screaming 'damage' like they do on lighter finishes.

What's the warranty situation, and does it actually matter?

Fully typically offers a 7-year warranty on the frame and motors, which is genuinely among the best in the category. Does it matter? Only if something actually fails—and the track record here suggests most units don't. But knowing you have that cushion reduces the anxiety of dropping this much money on office furniture.
