What to Look For

  • Lumbar Support That Actually Adjusts: Remote workers sit for longer stretches than office workers, so you need a chair with adjustable lumbar support—not just marketing language. Look for chairs where the lumbar curve can be moved up and down, or even better, forward and back. Your lower spine has a natural curve, and the chair should support it, not force you into an unnatural position.
  • Leather That Breathes and Ages Well: Real leather is durable and professional-looking, but cheap faux leather gets hot and sticky during long sessions. Full-grain or top-grain leather breathes better, won't crack after a year, and actually improves with age. Avoid bonded leather—it's the worst of both worlds and falls apart quickly.
  • Seat Height Range and Depth Control: Everyone's body is different. Your chair needs a wide height adjustment range (typically 17-21 inches) so your feet rest flat on the floor and your knees stay at 90 degrees. Seat depth matters too—deep enough to support your thighs but not so deep it cuts off circulation behind your knees.
