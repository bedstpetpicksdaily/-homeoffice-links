Frequently Asked Questions

What's the minimum weight capacity I need for dual monitors?

Your arm's rated capacity should exceed your combined monitor weight by at least 10–20 pounds. For example, if you're mounting two 27-inch monitors at ~7 lbs each (14 lbs total), look for an arm rated for at least 24–28 lbs per side. This buffer prevents sagging and creep over months of use.

Can I use these arms with different-sized monitors?

Yes. All three options support 17–32 inch displays with independent adjustment, so you can pair a 24-inch with a 32-inch without issue. The arm's weight capacity is what matters most, not matching monitor sizes.

Do these arms work with all desks?

They require a desk with a solid edge or clamping surface at least 1.5–2 inches thick. Flimsy particleboard desks under 1 inch may not grip securely. If your desk is thin, look for monitor arms with grommet-mount options (mount through a drilled hole) instead of clamps.

What's the difference between gas spring and spring-tension arms?

Gas spring arms (used in all three options here) stay where you position them without drifting or sagging. Spring-tension arms rely on counterbalancing weight, which can creep over time and requires periodic readjustment. Gas springs are worth the small price premium.

How often do I need to adjust a dual monitor arm?

Ideally, once during initial setup. However, over months, monitor weight can cause gradual creep downward. Quality gas springs (like Ergotron and HUANUO) resist this far better than budget options. Budget another 5-minute adjustment every 3–6 months if using AmazonBasics.
