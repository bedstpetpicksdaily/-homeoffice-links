What to Look For

  • Full-Range Motion and Easy Adjustment: Your monitor arm needs to move smoothly between sitting and standing positions without requiring tools or excessive force. Look for gas spring or spring-assisted mechanisms that let you adjust height, tilt, and rotation with one hand while holding your morning coffee in the other. The easier it is to reposition, the more likely you'll actually adjust it throughout the day instead of settling into an awkward fixed angle.
  • VESA Compatibility and Weight Capacity: Not all monitor arms fit all monitors. Verify that any arm you choose supports your specific monitor's VESA mounting pattern (typically 75x75mm or 100x100mm) and can handle your screen's weight. Undersized arms will sag over time, while oversized ones waste money and desk space. Check both the minimum and maximum monitor sizes supported—you want the arm perfectly matched to your setup.
  • Cable Management Integration: Standing desks require frequent height adjustments, which means your cables move constantly. The best monitor arms include built-in cable clips, channels, or sleeves that keep power cords and USB lines organized and prevent tangling. This prevents the frustration of cables catching during transitions and keeps your desk looking clean, which research shows contributes to better focus and reduced mental fatigue.

A quality monitor arm solves this by letting you move your screen smoothly between positions, ensuring your line of sight remains neutral throughout the day.     Do monitor arms work with standing desks?

    Yes, monitor arms are fully compatible with standing desks and are actually ideal for them since they free up desk space and allow you to adjust monitor height as you move between sitting and standing positions. Most monitor arms clamp to the desk edge or mount via grommet holes, making installation straightforward on both traditional and standing desk surfaces.

    What size monitor arm do I need for my standing desk?

    Monitor arms support different weight capacities—check your monitor's weight and choose an arm rated for at least that amount, typically ranging from 4-15 lbs for single monitors. Most standard 24-27 inch monitors (3-8 lbs) work with mid-range arms, while larger or dual-monitor setups require heavy-duty arms rated for 15+ lbs.

    How much desk space does a monitor arm save?

    A monitor arm typically saves 6-12 inches of desk depth by eliminating the monitor stand footprint, giving you valuable surface area for keyboards, notebooks, or other work materials. For remote workers with limited desk space, this can mean the difference between a cluttered and functional workspace.

    Can a monitor arm reduce neck and shoulder pain from my standing desk?

    Yes—a quality monitor arm lets you position your screen at eye level and at the correct distance (20-26 inches), which significantly reduces neck strain compared to fixed monitor stands. Proper ergonomics with an adjustable arm can eliminate the slouching and forward head posture that causes pain during long work sessions.
