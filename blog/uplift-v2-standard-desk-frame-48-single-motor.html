Frequently Asked Questions

Can the Uplift V2 Standard handle a standing desk converter on top of it?

Technically yes, but practically no—at least not well. The single motor is calibrated for the desktop weight you're expecting. Add a converter platform and you're asking the motor to lift extra load it wasn't designed for. You'll get slower adjustments, increased wear, and potential warranty issues. If you need flexibility, commit to either the motorized frame or a converter, not both.

How loud is the motor during height adjustment?

The motor runs at approximately 65-70 decibels according to user reports—about as loud as normal office conversation. It's not silent (dual motors sometimes are quieter), but it won't startle coworkers on video calls. Some users describe it as a subtle hum rather than a mechanical grinding noise. This is one of the areas where the single motor actually performs better than cheaper alternatives.

What's the actual weight capacity, and does it match what Uplift claims?

The frame typically supports 275-325 pounds depending on the exact model revision, and user reports consistently confirm this holds up in real conditions. The 4.3-star rating reflects that people aren't experiencing catastrophic failures when they hit stated limits. However, staying 25-30 pounds under the max is smart practice for long-term stability and motor longevity, especially if you're standing 6+ hours daily.
