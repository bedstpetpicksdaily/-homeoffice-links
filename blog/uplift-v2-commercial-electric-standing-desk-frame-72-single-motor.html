Frequently Asked Questions

Is a single motor really enough, or should I pay more for dual motors?

Single motors work fine for most people. The real question is how often you switch positions. If you're standing for 2-3 hours, then sitting for 4 hours, the single motor's 8-second transition won't bother you. If you're switching every 15 minutes, you'll get annoyed and likely stop using the standing feature entirely—in which case dual motors don't fix the behavior problem. Test your actual movement patterns before deciding.

Will this fit in a small home office, or is 72 inches too wide?

Seventy-two inches refers to the width of the desktop surface you mount on top, not the frame footprint itself. The frame legs sit inward, typically leaving 2-4 inches of overhang on each side. Measure your actual wall space and doorway width before ordering. If your desk sits against a corner or in a 60-inch-wide nook, this won't work. If you have standard home office space, 72 inches gives you genuine breathing room.

How much does this frame actually cost, and is it worth the price?

Prices vary by retailer and sales timing, but expect $400-$700 for the frame alone (desktop not included). Compare that to budget frames at $150-$250, and you're paying roughly double. The difference goes into motor reliability, frame rigidity, and weight capacity. If you're keeping this desk for 5+ years and use it daily, the durability premium pays for itself. If this is a trial purchase or you replace furniture every 2-3 years, the budget option makes more sense financially.

Is the Uplift V2 Commercial loud when moving up and down?

Single-motor frames are generally quieter than dual-motor models because there's one motor running instead of two competing. The Uplift V2 produces a moderate hum—audible in a quiet room but not loud enough to disrupt a video call if the microphone isn't pointed directly at the desk. If you're sound-sensitive or record audio frequently, test it in person first or check buyer videos on the product pages.

What desktop should I pair this with, and does it affect the total cost?

The frame is just the mechanism—you need your own desktop. Most people use solid wood (maple, walnut), butcher block, or quality laminate tops in 72-inch widths. Budget $200-$600 for a decent desktop depending on material and thickness. That means total desk cost runs $600-$1,300, which is important context for the price discussion. Don't assume you're only paying for the frame.
