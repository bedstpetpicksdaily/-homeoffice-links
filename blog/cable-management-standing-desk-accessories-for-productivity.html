What to Look For

  • Height-Adjustable Compatibility: Your cable management system needs to move seamlessly with your desk as you transition between sitting and standing positions. Look for cable clips, trays, and organizers specifically designed for moving desks that won't create tension or damage cables during adjustments.
  • Accessibility and Flexibility: You should be able to add, remove, or reroute cables without completely deconstructing your setup. Modular systems with adhesive clips, magnetic attachments, or tool-free adjustments save you time and frustration when you need to swap equipment or troubleshoot connections.
  • Cable Capacity and Organization: Consider how many cables you actually have—monitor cables, USB hubs, keyboard, mouse, chargers, and peripherals add up quickly. Choose accessories with dedicated channels, separate compartments, or color-coding options that keep different cable types organized and easy to identify at a glance.

What to Avoid

  • Rigid Fixed Cable Trays: Heavy-duty trays designed for stationary desks will fight against your desk's height adjustments and can eventually damage both the tray and your cables. Your standing desk's movement is a feature, not a bug—don't use accessories that ignore this fundamental characteristic.
  • Cable Ties and Velcro Straps: While these are inexpensive, they require you to completely unbundle and retie cables every time you want to add equipment or adjust your setup. This friction discourages you from making ergonomic adjustments, defeating the whole purpose of owning a standing desk.
  • Minimalist "Wireless-Only" Approach: While wireless peripherals are great, most home office setups require some cables for monitors, power, and network connections. Ignoring these cables in hopes they'll manage themselves leads to the tangled mess you're trying to avoid in the first place.
