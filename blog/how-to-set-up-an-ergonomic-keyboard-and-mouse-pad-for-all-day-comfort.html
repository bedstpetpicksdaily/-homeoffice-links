What to Look For

  • Negative tilt and wrist support: Your keyboard should be positioned with a slight negative tilt (keys lower toward you than away from you) to keep your wrists in a neutral, straight position. Look for keyboards with built-in palm rests or pair yours with a dedicated keyboard wrist rest that provides firm but cushioned support without being too thick.
  • Mouse pad with dedicated wrist support: A standard thin mouse pad won't cut it for all-day use. Choose a mouse pad with an integrated wrist rest that's at least 1.5 inches thick and made from memory foam or gel. This keeps your wrist elevated and properly aligned with your forearm, preventing the downward angle that causes strain.
  • Proper positioning height and distance: Your keyboard and mouse pad should sit at a height where your elbows are at 90 degrees and your forearms are parallel to the ground. The entire setup should be 24-28 inches from the floor, depending on your desk height and chair. Both peripherals should be close together to minimize reach and shoulder strain.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What height should my keyboard and mouse be when sitting at a desk?

    Your keyboard and mouse should be positioned at elbow height when your arms are relaxed at your sides, typically 28-30 inches from the floor depending on your chair height. Your elbows should form a 90-degree angle, and your wrists should remain neutral and straight, not bent up or down. Adjust your chair or desk height first, then position your keyboard and mouse accordingly.

    Do I really need a keyboard and mouse pad or is it just marketing?

    Yes, ergonomic pads are genuinely beneficial—they provide wrist support and reduce strain on your tendons during all-day typing and clicking. A quality pad with proper cushioning and wrist rest can significantly reduce repetitive strain injuries and carpal tunnel pain, especially if you're working 8+ hours daily. Budget $25-50 for a decent pad that will last years and prevent costly medical issues.

    What's the best way to position my mouse pad relative to my keyboard?

    Your mouse pad should be at the same height as your keyboard with the wrist rest aligned so your wrist stays neutral when reaching for the mouse. Position it directly beside your keyboard (not too far to the side) so you're not overextending your arm, and keep your mouse close to your body rather than at arm's length. Your elbow should stay relatively static while only your forearm and wrist move when using the mouse.

    What's the difference between a keyboard pad and a mouse pad, and do I need both?

    A keyboard pad supports your wrists while typing, while a mouse pad with wrist rest supports your wrist during mouse use—they serve different purposes and you'll benefit from having both. Some all-in-one ergonomic solutions combine a large extended pad for both keyboard and mouse, which can be more convenient and space-efficient. Choose based on your desk space and whether you want matching support on both sides of your workstation.
