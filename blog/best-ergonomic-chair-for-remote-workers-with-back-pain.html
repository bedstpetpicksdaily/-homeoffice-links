What to Look For

  • Lumbar support that actually fits your spine: Look for chairs with adjustable lumbar support that can be positioned to support the natural curve of your lower back. Generic lumbar pillows won't cut it—you need support that moves with you and can be fine-tuned to your specific spinal curve. This is non-negotiable if you're dealing with existing back pain.
  • Seat depth and width that accommodates long sitting sessions: A chair that's too deep will put pressure on the backs of your thighs; too shallow and you won't have proper support. For back pain sufferers, you need a seat that allows you to sit fully back against the backrest while maintaining 2-3 inches between the seat edge and the back of your knees. This positioning ensures your spine stays properly supported throughout the day.
  • Height adjustability and armrests that reduce shoulder strain: Your feet should rest flat on the ground with knees at 90 degrees, and your armrests should be at elbow height. Many remote workers with back pain actually have tension stemming from poor arm positioning, which radiates down the spine. Adjustable armrests are critical because they take load off your shoulders and neck, which directly impacts your lower back.
