Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Mirra 2 StudioEdition compare to the original Mirra or Aeron?

The Mirra 2 is lighter and more affordable than the Aeron ($1,695+) while keeping most ergonomic features intact. Versus the original Mirra, you get better PostureFit lumbar support and improved arm mechanisms. If budget is tight, the original Mirra often drops to $900-1,100 used or refurbished—a solid middle ground.

Is the StudioEdition worth it compared to standard Mirra 2?

The StudioEdition adds upholstered seat cushioning and refined aesthetics, making it more residential-friendly. If your home office is visible on video calls, the StudioEdition justifies the extra $200-300. For hidden setups, standard Mirra 2 delivers identical ergonomics at lower cost.

What's the real-world break-even point for buying this instead of a $600 chair?

Roughly 4-5 years of daily use. If you work from home full-time and keep the chair for a decade (realistic with Herman Miller's durability), you're spending $150/year versus $200-300/year replacing cheaper chairs every 2-3 years. However, if you return to an office in 18 months, you've overpaid significantly.
