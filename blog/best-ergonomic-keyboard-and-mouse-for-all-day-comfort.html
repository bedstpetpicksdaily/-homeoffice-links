What to Look For

  • Wrist Support and Neutral Positioning: Look for keyboards with a slight negative tilt (keys lower at the back) and mice with built-in palm rests or contoured designs that keep your wrist in a straight, neutral line. This positioning reduces strain on tendons and nerves that can lead to carpal tunnel syndrome.
  • Responsive Keys and Smooth Tracking: For keyboards, prioritize models with low actuation force and tactile feedback that doesn't require you to bottom out each key. For mice, choose one with precise tracking, adjustable DPI settings, and smooth gliding to minimize repetitive micro-movements that cause fatigue.
  • Portability and Adjustability (If Flexible): If you transition between sitting and standing throughout the day, consider wireless options that move easily between desk heights, or look for mice with adjustable sensitivity that adapts to different working positions and distances.
