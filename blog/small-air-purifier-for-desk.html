What to Look For

1. True HEPA Filtration

Look for "True HEPA" certification specifically—this means it captures 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns or larger. This includes pollen, dust, pet dander, and mold spores. Avoid "HEPA-style" filters, which don't meet official standards and won't deliver the same results.

2. Coverage Area (CADR Rating)

Check the Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) number. For a desk, you want a unit rated for 50-100 square feet minimum. Higher CADR means faster air cycling, so you get cleaner air in the immediate workspace more quickly. This is especially important in smaller home offices.

3. Quiet Operation (Under 50dB)

You'll be sitting next to this for hours. Anything above 50dB (normal conversation level) becomes distracting during calls and focused work. Look for models specifically designed for quiet operation on lower fan speeds—you don't need maximum power running all day.

4. Filter Replacement Costs & Lifespan

A cheap unit means expensive filters. Check the replacement filter price (expect $20-50) and lifespan (typically 6-12 months depending on use). Calculate annual filter costs before buying—this ongoing expense matters more than the initial purchase price.
