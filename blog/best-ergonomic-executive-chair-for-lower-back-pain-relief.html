What to Look For

  • Adjustable Lumbar Support: Look for chairs with independently adjustable lumbar support—not just a fixed curve. Your lower back has a unique shape, and a one-size-fits-all approach won't work. The best chairs let you customize both the height and depth of lumbar support to match your spine's natural curve.
  • Seat Depth and Height Adjustability: A proper seat should allow your feet to rest flat on the floor with your thighs parallel to the ground. Adjustable seat depth (typically 16-18 inches) ensures your knees don't press against the edge, which can restrict blood flow and aggravate lower back pain.
  • High-Density Foam and Proper Recline: Dense cushioning (3-4 inches minimum) prevents you from bottoming out after a few months of use. Look for a chair with a synchronized tilt mechanism that supports your back as you recline, maintaining lumbar support rather than forcing you into an unsupported slouch.
