Frequently Asked Questions

Will this actually help with my neck and shoulder pain?

Yes, if your pain is from monitor height. Most people unconsciously tilt their heads down to look at screens sitting at desk level, which creates tension from your shoulders up through your neck. Raising your monitor to eye level (top of screen at or slightly below eye height when sitting) removes that strain. You'll notice the difference within 2-3 days. That said, this isn't a substitute for taking breaks or getting proper posture in your chair—but it's the first physical adjustment that actually matters.

How much weight can the storage compartment hold?

Realistically, 5-10 pounds before you're pushing your luck. The plastic is durable but not industrial-grade. Papers and light office supplies are the intended use case. Don't try to store textbooks or your entire printer paper stash underneath. If you load it smart (lighter items in the front, heavier stuff on your actual desk), it's more than enough for a typical home office workflow.

Is this better than just using books or a monitor arm?

Versus books: way better. Books slide, books wear out, books look like you gave up on your setup. This is designed for the job. Versus a monitor arm: different use case. An arm gives you height + depth + tilt adjustments and looks cleaner. This riser gives you static height + storage + zero learning curve. If you want flexibility, go with an arm. If you want simple, cheap, and it-just-works, this riser wins. The $10 price point also means you can upgrade to an arm later without feeling wasteful.
