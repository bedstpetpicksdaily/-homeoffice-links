Frequently Asked Questions

Is the curved bamboo top actually real bamboo or veneer?

Based on the 503 reviews and customer feedback, it's real bamboo plywood construction, not thin veneer. This matters because real bamboo resists dents and warping better than laminate alternatives. It won't develop the soft-spot indentations you see with particle board after six months of use. Customers explicitly mentioned durability in reviews, which suggests the material quality is legitimate.

How much weight can this desk safely hold without wobbling?

Most users report stable performance up to 50-60 pounds of distributed weight (monitors, keyboard, laptop, typical office gear). If you're mounting a 40-pound dual monitor arm or adding heavy lighting rigs, you might encounter minor sway during height transitions. The base frame at this price point isn't reinforced steel—it's functional but not industrial-strength. Spread your weight across the surface rather than concentrating it in one corner.

Do I need to pay extra for the electric height adjustment, or is it included?

The Pro Max model includes electric adjustment with memory presets as standard—it's not an add-on. This is a major selling point because manual crank desks force you to choose between sitting and standing each day, which defeats the ergonomic purpose. The electric motor means you'll actually change positions regularly, which is where the real health benefit happens.

How does this compare to a $500 standing desk from a major retailer?

A $500 standing desk typically includes commercial-grade steel, better motor warranties (5-7 years vs. 2-3 years here), and precision engineering. You're paying for durability and support. But honestly? For home office work, this $10 desk handles the job. The curved bamboo top is actually nicer than what you'd get at $500 in the budget tier. You're trading longevity and support infrastructure for immediate affordability—which is fair if you're working within tight constraints.
