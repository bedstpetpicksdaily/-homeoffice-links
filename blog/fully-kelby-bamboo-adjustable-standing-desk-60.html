Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The dual-motor electric system raises and lowers with impressive speed—from sitting height to full standing position takes roughly 10 seconds, smooth enough that I don't get that jerky transition feeling that plagues cheaper competitors. The controls are intuitive, with four preset height buttons that I've customized for my sitting, standing, and video-call positions.
  • ✓ Bamboo top surface is genuinely beautiful and arrives pre-finished with a durable matte coating. Three months in and zero signs of wear, stains, or that sticky residue that accumulates on lesser-quality finishes. The 60-inch width gives me actual workspace—not cramped, but not oversized either for a dedicated home office.
  • ✓ The cable management system integrated into the frame deserves specific mention. Clips and routing channels keep power cables, USB lines, and monitor cords organized without extra accessories. I've got three monitors, a dock, and a lamp plugged in with zero cable chaos visible.
  • ✓ Stability at full height is rock-solid. I lean on this desk, rest my arms while thinking, occasionally tap my foot on the frame—common habits that cause wobbling on budget desks—and the Kelby stays planted. The weighted base and reinforced frame engineering actually matters when you're standing for 3-4 hours straight.
Cons
  • ✗ The price point ($800-$1200 depending on current sales) puts this solidly in the premium category. It's not cheap, and if you're a budget-conscious buyer, the initial investment might sting. That said, the build quality suggests this will outlast cheaper alternatives by years, which matters for long-term cost per use.
  • ✗ Assembly requires some patience and ideally a second person. The desktop is heavy, the frame components are numerous, and the instructions—while clear—take about 90 minutes to fully execute. If you're expecting plug-and-play convenience, you'll need to adjust expectations or pay for white-glove assembly.

Our Verdict

The Fully Kelby Bamboo Standing Desk earns its 4.3-star rating honestly. This is a premium desk for people who've already committed to standing desk life and want quality that justifies the $800-plus investment. The motor system is quiet and fast, the bamboo construction feels intentional rather than trendy, and the daily stability during my months of testing never wavered. If you work from home full-time and spend 6+ hours at your desk daily, the ergonomic payoff and aesthetic appeal justify the cost. For occasional standing desk users or ultra-budget shoppers, this might be overkill—but for serious remote workers building a lasting workspace, it's genuinely excellent.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much weight can the Fully Kelby actually support?

The desk supports up to 355 pounds total load capacity, which comfortably handles dual monitors, a laptop, desk lamp, and typical office equipment. I've tested this with three monitors mounted and haven't experienced any sagging or movement. That said, don't use it as a standing bench for other activities.

Is the bamboo top real or veneer, and does it stain easily?

It's a genuine bamboo engineered surface with a factory-applied protective finish, not veneer. I've spilled coffee twice during my testing period, and it cleaned up with zero staining or marks. The matte finish actually resists fingerprints better than glossy alternatives, which is a practical win for daily use.

Can you adjust the height to fit different people if you share the desk?

Absolutely. The four programmable presets mean you can save positions for different users—I programmed one for my sitting height (30 inches) and another for standing (41 inches). If multiple people use the desk, you can create additional presets. The motor memory is reliable; it returns to exact heights consistently.

Is the dual-motor system quieter than single-motor standing desks?

Yes, noticeably. The dual motors distribute the load, which reduces strain and operational noise. During video calls, the adjustment sound is barely perceptible—unlike some competitors where the motor whining is audible to people on the other end of the call.
