What to Look For

  • Adjustable height and lumbar support: Your desk should allow you to position your monitor at eye level while keeping your elbows at 90 degrees. Equally important is a chair with dedicated lumbar support that matches your spine's natural curve—this is non-negotiable when you already have back pain, as a generic chair will only aggravate your condition.
  • Keyboard and mouse positioning: Look for a setup where your keyboard tray or desk surface allows your wrists to stay neutral and your shoulders relaxed. A misaligned keyboard position forces your back to compensate by twisting or leaning, which directly increases pain—so this detail matters more than you might think.
  • The ability to alternate positions: Since sitting in any position for 8+ hours causes strain, prioritize a setup that lets you switch between sitting and standing throughout the day. A height-adjustable desk paired with a supportive chair gives you the flexibility to move when pain signals appear.
