What to Look For

Blue Light Filtering Technology

Not all "blue light blocking" lamps are created equal. Look for lamps that specifically filter 30-50% of blue light at full brightness, with the ability to shift to warmer color temperatures (3000K or lower) for evening use. Check for third-party testing or certifications—reputable brands will provide spectral data showing their filtration effectiveness.

Tunable Brightness & Color Temperature

A true anti-glare solution needs both adjustable brightness AND color temperature control. You should be able to dim to 20% for late-night work and shift from cool white (6000K+) during focus hours to warm amber (2700K) in the evening. Look for at least 3-5 preset modes or smooth sliding controls for maximum flexibility.

Flicker-Free, Dimmable LED Technology

Cheap desk lamps with flickering or PWM (pulse-width modulation) dimming aggravate eye strain despite having brightness controls. Insist on lamps labeled "flicker-free" and "high-frequency PWM" (above 30kHz) or constant-current dimming technology. Your eyes won't consciously see the flickering, but your brain will register the strain.

Ergonomic Design & Even Light Distribution

An anti-glare lamp must actually prevent glare while eliminating harsh shadows. Look for matte diffusion panels, adjustable arms (30-50 degree range), and even illumination across your entire desk surface. The best designs use wider LED panels rather than focused point sources, distributing light naturally without creating bright hotspots.
