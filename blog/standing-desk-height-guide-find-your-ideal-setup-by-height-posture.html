Standing desk height isn't one-size-fits-all. While manufacturers often default to 28–30 inches, your ideal height depends on your individual measurements—primarily your elbow height when standing naturally. Get this wrong and you'll face neck strain, shoulder pain, and wrist issues. Get it right, and you unlock genuine ergonomic comfort.

Measure your current desk height and compare it to the recommended range for your height category before purchasing, as improper standing desk height can lead to shoulder strain and reduced productivity despite the desk's quality features. Check whether the desk's adjustability range covers your specific height needs, especially if you're significantly taller or shorter than average, to ensure it supports both sitting and standing positions comfortably.

Adjustable Manual Crank Standing Desks

Manual-crank adjustable desks offer the same range flexibility (typically 22–48 inches) as electric models but require hand-cranking to change height. They're generally more affordable than motorized versions and have fewer mechanical parts to fail.

The trade-off: manual adjustment discourages frequent position changes, so users often set a height and leave it there. This works if you primarily stand or primarily sit, but undermines the health benefits of alternating postures.

Best for: Budget-conscious buyers who plan to use one fixed posture most of the day, or those in offices where frequent adjustments aren't practical.

Fixed-Height Standing Desks (28–30 inches)

Fixed-height desks are locked at a standard height, typically 28–30 inches, matching the ergonomic benchmark for average adults (5'8"–6'0"). They're simpler to manufacture, more affordable, and have no mechanical failure points.

However, fixed desks don't work for sitting, and they fail for anyone significantly taller or shorter than the average range. Verify that your body measurements actually align with the fixed height before committing.

Best for: Standing-only workspaces, users within the average height range who don't need to sit, or anyone seeking maximum stability and simplicity.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my ideal standing desk height?

Stand upright with your shoes on. Bend your arms to 90 degrees and let them hang naturally at your sides. Measure the distance from the floor to your elbows. That measurement is your ideal desk height. Alternatively, multiply your height by 0.62 as a rough approximation, then verify with the physical measurement.

Can one desk height work for both sitting and standing?

No. Sitting typically requires 28–30 inches; standing requires a height matching your individual elbow measurement (often 28–36+ inches depending on height). Adjustable electric desks solve this by letting you switch between two preset heights. Fixed desks force you to choose one posture.

What if I'm very tall (over 6'2") or very short (under 5'6")?

Standard desks at 28–30 inches won't work. Verify that any desk you're considering has an adjustment range that covers your elbow height. Most quality adjustable desks span 22–48 inches, which accommodates most extreme heights. Fixed-height desks are a poor fit; prioritize adjustable models.

How far should my monitor be from my eyes?

Position your monitor 20–26 inches away from your eyes (roughly arm's length), with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level (about 15–20 degrees downward). This prevents neck strain and eye fatigue. Screen height matters as much as desk height for overall posture.

What's the difference between electric and manual crank standing desks?

Electric desks adjust at the push of a button and save presets, encouraging frequent posture changes. Manual cranks require hand-cranking to adjust, which discourages switching. Electric is more durable long-term and justifies the higher cost ($400+) if you plan to alternate between sitting and standing daily. Manual desks work if you're committing to one posture.

Should I buy a standing desk or an ergonomic office chair?

Both serve different needs. An adjustable standing desk (like Sunaofe) lets you switch postures throughout the day, reducing circulation issues and strain from prolonged sitting. An ergonomic office chair (like COLAMY at $200+) improves seated comfort but doesn't address the posture variety benefit. If standing is part of your plan, prioritize the standing desk. If you're seated full-time, a good chair is your best investment.
