What to Look For

  • Adjustable height and tilt: Your keyboard tray should move independently of your desk surface, allowing you to position it at elbow height whether you're sitting or standing. Look for trays with multiple tilt angles so your wrists stay neutral—typically a slight negative tilt (sloping downward away from you) works best for most people.
  • Smooth swivel and rotation: A quality keyboard tray needs to glide effortlessly and rotate to accommodate different work positions throughout your day. This flexibility prevents you from locking into a single static posture, which is exactly what standing desks are designed to help you avoid.
  • Sturdy mounting with minimal wobble: The best keyboard tray won't help if it shifts or bounces when you type. Check mounting mechanisms that clamp securely to your desk and use quality ball bearings or tracks—flimsy trays create tension as you unconsciously brace against movement.
