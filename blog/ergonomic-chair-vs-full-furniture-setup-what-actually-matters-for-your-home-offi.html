Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I actually spend on an ergonomic chair?

COLAMY's $200+ range represents solid entry-level ergonomic seating with adjustable lumbar support. Higher-end chairs add more adjustment axes, better materials, and longer warranties, but the law of diminishing returns kicks in above $400 for most home office users. If you're also buying a desk system (Sunaofe approach), your chair budget becomes part of a larger equation.

What's the difference between a basic chair and a real ergonomic chair?

A basic chair has a fixed backrest and seat height; an ergonomic chair allows customization of lumbar support curve, seat depth, seat width, armrest height, and tilt tension. COLAMY specifically emphasizes adjustable lumbar support and seat dimensions because these prevent pressure on nerves and blood vessels during long work sessions. A chair that doesn't fit your body dimensions fails regardless of other features.

Is a sit-stand desk worth the investment if I already have a good chair?

Not immediately. If you've just upgraded to a quality ergonomic chair and your pain decreases, sitting 8 hours daily still causes strain. Standing desks (part of Sunaofe's offerings) reduce this by allowing position variation, but they're most valuable after you've solved your seating problem. Add one later if sitting-only pain returns.

How do I know if an ergonomic chair will actually fit my body?

Test seat depth (your thighs should be fully supported without pressure on the back of your knees) and seat width (hips should fit comfortably without pinching). COLAMY emphasizes these because they're the most commonly ignored dimensions. If you can't test in person, measure your thigh length and hip width, then compare to the chair's specifications.

Should I wait for a complete Sunaofe system or buy COLAMY now?

If you're experiencing pain now, buy the COLAMY chair immediately—pain relief shouldn't wait for budget accumulation. A good chair takes effect within days. Once seated comfort improves, reassess whether monitor height, desk positioning, or standing options address remaining issues. This phased approach also reveals what you actually need rather than buying a complete system with unused features.
