What to Look For

1. Vertical Stacking Capability

Not all dual monitor stands stack vertically equally. Look for stands specifically designed with vertical orientation in mind—meaning the monitors are mounted one directly above the other rather than at slight angles. This maximizes your space savings. Check product images carefully to confirm the stand supports true vertical alignment, not just a tilted arrangement.

2. Weight Capacity and Stability

Vertical stacking puts more stress on the base. You need a stand that supports at least 25-30 lbs per monitor (preferably 35+ lbs for future upgrades). Look for weighted bases with non-slip feet, or stands with multiple mounting points to a desk clamp. This is non-negotiable—a wobbling monitor is both unsafe and productivity-killing.

3. Adjustability for Ergonomics

Just because you're going vertical doesn't mean you should sacrifice ergonomics. The best stands offer tilt, rotation, and height adjustments so you can position both monitors at eye level (or close to it). Swivel functionality is a bonus if you work with multiple people or need to share your screen frequently.

4. Monitor Compatibility and Flexibility

Check that the stand accommodates your specific monitor sizes and VESA mounting patterns (75x75mm or 100x100mm are most common). Some stands are locked into specific monitor sizes; the best ones work with a range from 24" to 32" monitors. If you might upgrade monitors later, flexibility matters.
