Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Fully Cooper Pro compare to cheaper standing desks under $400?

Budget standing desks get the job done—motors work, desks go up and down. Where you notice the difference is smoothness, noise level, and longevity. The Cooper Pro's dual motors move faster and quieter, and the solid walnut top won't sag or warp after a few years of use. Cheaper desks often feel plasticky and develop squeaks. That said, if standing desks are new to you, test the waters with a budget model first. Many people buy them and never actually stand.

Is the 72-inch width really necessary, or can I save money going smaller?

Depends on your setup. If you're running dual 27-inch monitors, a laptop, and need actual desk space? You'll want the full 72 inches. If you're a single-monitor, minimalist type, a 48-60 inch desk works fine. The width isn't just about fitting more stuff—it's about not feeling cramped. Cramped workspaces create tension, which defeats the entire purpose of a standing desk. Measure your current setup before deciding.

Will the walnut top scratch easily, and is it worth the maintenance hassle?

Walnut is softer than oak or maple, so yes, it scratches more easily. A mouse pad and keyboard tray help protect it. Maintenance isn't difficult—dust weekly, condition every 3-4 months, avoid spills near the grain. If that sounds annoying, go with a laminated surface instead. The maintenance trade-off is worth it only if you actually care how your desk looks after year two. If you don't, save the money and go synthetic.
