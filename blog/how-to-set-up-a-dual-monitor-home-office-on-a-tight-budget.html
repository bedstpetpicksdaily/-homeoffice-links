What to Look For

  • Refurbished or Previous-Generation Monitors (22-24 inches): Skip the latest 4K displays and opt for certified refurbished 1080p monitors from reputable retailers. These typically cost $80-$150 each and perform perfectly for email, spreadsheets, and web browsing. A 24-inch 1080p monitor offers excellent screen real estate without the premium price tag of newer models.
  • A Simple Monitor Arm or Dual Monitor Stand (under $50): Rather than investing in premium ergonomic arms, look for basic VESA-compatible monitor arms on budget retailers. They achieve the same height adjustment and space-saving benefits as expensive alternatives. Even a basic dual monitor stand ($30-$40) frees up valuable desk space compared to using monitor bases.
  • A Compact Desk (48-60 inches) Instead of Premium Standing Desks: A solid fixed-height desk from budget furniture retailers ($150-$250) is more practical than a motorized standing desk when funds are tight. You can always add a standing desk converter ($50-$100) later to introduce movement without the initial $800+ investment in a full electric desk.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What's the cheapest way to set up dual monitors for work from home?

    Buy two used or refurbished monitors from Facebook Marketplace, eBay, or Best Buy's outlet section—you can often find 24-inch monitors for $80-150 each. Pair them with a basic dual monitor stand ($20-40) or VESA mount arms, and use your existing desk space by positioning them side-by-side or stacking them vertically.

    Do I need a new graphics card for dual monitors?

    Most modern laptops and desktops already have enough video outputs (HDMI, DisplayPort, or USB-C) to support two monitors without upgrading. If your computer only has one video port, a USB-to-HDMI adapter ($15-30) is much cheaper than a new graphics card.

    Can I use two different sized monitors together?

    Yes, you can absolutely mix monitor sizes—many remote workers pair a 24-inch main monitor with a 21-inch secondary one. Just adjust your monitor heights and positions in Windows/Mac settings under display preferences so the cursor moves smoothly between them.

    What monitor size is best for a small desk with a tight budget?

    Two 24-inch monitors are the budget sweet spot—they cost $100-180 each used, fit most desks, and provide enough screen real estate for email, spreadsheets, and video calls without requiring a huge workspace. If space is really tight, 22-inch monitors or a single ultrawide can be cheaper alternatives.
