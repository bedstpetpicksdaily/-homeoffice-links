The Short Answer

Ergonomic keyboards—particularly split or curved designs—paired with vertical or contoured mice are most effective for preventing wrist pain. These specialized peripherals maintain your wrist in a neutral position rather than the pronated (twisted) position created by standard keyboards. Adding wrist rests and adjusting your desk height to keep elbows at 90 degrees completes the solution.

The Full Explanation

Wrist pain from keyboard and mouse use typically stems from repetitive strain injury (RSI), carpal tunnel syndrome, or general muscle fatigue. Standard keyboards force your wrists into an unnatural curved position, while flat mice require awkward pronation movements. Over hours of daily typing and clicking, this compounds into chronic pain.

Ergonomic Keyboard Features: Split keyboards separate the key layout into two angled sections, allowing each hand to maintain a neutral wrist position without twisting. Curved keyboards follow a gentler arc that accommodates natural hand width. Cushioned wrist rests provide support during extended typing sessions, reducing pressure on the carpal tunnel. Mechanical keyboards with lighter key actuation require less finger force, decreasing overall strain.

Ergonomic Mouse Characteristics: Vertical mice position your hand as if shaking hands rather than lying flat, eliminating pronation stress. Contoured mice with thumb supports distribute pressure more evenly across your hand. Adjustable mice let you customize height and angle for your specific grip. Trackballs and vertical designs reduce the repetitive clicking motion that strains the wrist and forearm.

Why Standard Peripherals Cause Pain: Traditional flat keyboards angle your wrists upward (extension) and inward (ulnar deviation), compressing nerves and tendons. Standard mice force constant pronation and repetitive clicking at angles that stress the wrist extensors. After eight hours daily, these biomechanical stresses accumulate into pain, numbness, and reduced mobility.

What the Experts Say

Physical therapists and occupational health specialists consistently recommend ergonomic keyboards and mice as primary interventions for RSI prevention. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons emphasizes that proper equipment setup prevents conditions from developing in the first place, making it more cost-effective than treatment later. Ergonomic researchers have documented that split keyboards reduce wrist extension by up to 20 degrees compared to standard boards, while vertical mice eliminate pronation entirely—measurable biomechanical improvements that translate to reduced pain.
