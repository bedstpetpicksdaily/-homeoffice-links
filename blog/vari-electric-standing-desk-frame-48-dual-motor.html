Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Vari 48" dual motor frame worth it compared to single-motor standing desks?

Depends on your load and stability priorities. Dual motors excel when you're stacking monitors, printers, and heavy equipment on your desk. Single-motor frames handle lighter setups just fine and cost $200-400 less. If you're using just a laptop and one monitor, single-motor saves money without sacrificing functionality. But if you're serious about a permanent standing desk with multiple displays, the dual-motor stability is worth the premium.

How stable is this frame when fully raised to standing height?

Very stable. The dual motor design means weight distributes evenly across both sides, preventing the wobble problem that plagues cheaper single-motor frames. Real buyers report minimal sway even with full desk loads extended to maximum height. That said, it's not military-grade stability—extremely heavy shock loads can create minor movement, but normal workday use shows zero issues.

Do I really need preset memory buttons, or is that just marketing?

Genuine quality-of-life feature, not marketing fluff. Without memory buttons, you're manually adjusting height every time you switch positions, which gets tedious fast. Most people establish two positions (sitting height and standing height) and use them repeatedly. Buttons make this instant instead of requiring fine-tuning. If you're serious about actually alternating between positions throughout your day, this matters. If you sit for six hours then stand for one, skip it.
