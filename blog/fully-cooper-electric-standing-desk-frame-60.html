Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Fully Cooper compare to Flexispot or Uplift for the price?

The Cooper competes directly with the Flexispot E7 and Uplift V2. You'll pay roughly the same amount ($400-600), but the Cooper's advantage is its size—60" is ideal for standard home offices. The Uplift V2 edges ahead on warranty length and motor quietness, while Flexispot undercuts slightly on price. None of these three are objectively 'better'—it depends whether you value brand reputation (Uplift), price savings (Flexispot), or size fit (Cooper).

Is the dual motor really necessary or is it just marketing?

Dual motors aren't marketing fluff. They distribute weight more evenly, so your desktop won't tilt when you raise it unevenly (like if a monitor arm pulls on one side). They're also noticeably quieter—single-motor frames sound like angry calculators. For a 60" desk supporting dual monitors, dual motors prevent the wobble that'll drive you crazy by week three.

What desktop should I pair with the Cooper frame?

Avoid flimsy particle board or thin plywood—they'll sag within a year, especially when raising and lowering constantly. Budget at least $150-200 for a solid desktop (4cm solid wood, quality laminate, or engineered wood with decent thickness). IKEA Karlby or Gerton work fine if you're watching pennies. Higher-end options like Fully's own desktop or Autonomous desktops cost more but look better and last longer.

How much weight can it actually hold?

Most Fully Cooper frames support around 150-180 lbs total (frame + desktop + equipment). That's fine for standard setups—two monitors, a laptop, a lamp, and some clutter. If you're planning to load it with three monitors, a printer, and stacks of filing cabinets, verify the exact specs before buying. The 500+ reviews suggest most people stay well within limits without issue.
