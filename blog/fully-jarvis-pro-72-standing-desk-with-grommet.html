Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Fully Jarvis Pro+ compare to the Uplift V2 or Flexispot E7?

All three have dual motors and solid frames, but they prioritize differently. The Uplift V2 ($500-600) offers nearly identical stability and memory presets at a lower price—you're mainly paying extra for Jarvis Pro+'s bamboo aesthetic and slightly more refined cable management. The Flexispot E7 ($450-550) is the budget win here, though it uses a slightly smaller motor and lacks the premium feel. If budget is your constraint, the Flexispot handles daily standing desk duties just fine. If you want the nicest-looking desk and don't mind paying for it, Jarvis edges ahead.

Is the 72" width actually necessary, or should I downsize?

Honestly? Most people don't need 72 inches. A single monitor setup works fine at 48-60 inches; dual monitors fit comfortably at 60 inches; triple monitors or extreme peripheral arrays benefit from 72 inches. If you're downsizing to a 60" version, you'd save $150-250 while keeping all the core features. Do a measurement test: tape off your current workspace and see what width you actually use. You'll probably surprise yourself.

Is the bamboo top worth the extra $200+ over laminate?

If you keep furniture long-term (5+ years), yes. Bamboo resists scratches, stains, and daily wear better than particle board with laminate veneer. Bamboo feels premium and ages gracefully. If you're the type to upgrade desks every 3-4 years or don't care about aesthetics, laminate saves real money with minimal functional difference. For a home office you'll spend 40+ hours per week at, the premium top is reasonable—you're touching it constantly and staring at it daily.
