How does the Autonomous SmartDesk Core compare to cheaper manual crank alternatives?

Electric motors cost more upfront but you'll actually use the standing function because it takes three seconds instead of thirty. The health ROI flips in favor of the electric model around month four when you're not just sitting all day. If you genuinely commit to switching positions hourly, manual works. Does the 48-inch width feel cramped compared to 60-inch desks?

Depends entirely on your setup. A 27-inch monitor plus a 13-inch laptop fits comfortably with room for a notebook and mouse. Two 27-inch monitors sit side-by-side but tight. Three monitors? You'll hate it. If your work involves primarily one large screen or a monitor-plus-laptop combo, 48 inches is genuinely sufficient and saves floor space in smaller home offices. The 48-inch option also costs $100-150 less than wider versions, which matters when you're already buying the desktop separately.

Are the four memory presets actually useful, or is that just marketing?

Legitimately useful, especially if you share the desk with a partner or alternate between sitting and standing throughout the day. Without presets, you're eyeballing heights or constantly adjusting. With presets, you hit a button and spend zero mental energy on positioning. Most users report they use preset switching within two weeks—it becomes automatic. If you're the only person using the desk and only switch between one sitting and one standing height, a two-preset model saves $50-75, but the extra presets don't cost much more, so keeping the option is practical.
