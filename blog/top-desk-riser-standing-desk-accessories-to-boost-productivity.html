What to Look For

  • Adjustable Height and Stability: Your desk riser should accommodate your monitor at eye level whether you're sitting or standing, and it must be rock-solid stable. Wobbly risers break your focus and can damage equipment. Look for risers with a wide, weighted base and smooth height adjustment mechanisms that hold their position without slipping.
  • Cable Management Integration: A cluttered desk kills productivity faster than anything else. Choose risers with built-in cable trays, clips, or routing channels that keep cords organized and out of sight. This single feature prevents the constant visual distraction that drains mental energy throughout the day.
  • Material Quality and Workspace Preservation: Your riser should complement your desk surface without scratching or damaging it. Metal bases with rubber feet, tempered glass tops, or bamboo construction offer durability while protecting your investment and maintaining a professional appearance that supports focus.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What desk riser height should I get for my standing desk?

    The ideal desk riser height depends on your current desk height and monitor size, but most people use risers between 4-6 inches to achieve proper eye level when standing. Your monitor should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level when you're standing upright with shoulders relaxed, typically 20-26 inches above your work surface.

    Are desk risers bad for your back or posture?

    Desk risers themselves aren't bad for your back when used correctly—they actually improve posture by raising your monitor to eye level and reducing neck strain. However, using a riser without adjusting your monitor height or standing for too long without breaks can cause discomfort, so pair your riser with an ergonomic monitor arm and remember to alternate between sitting and standing.

    Can a desk riser work with a monitor arm or do I need both?

    You can use either a desk riser alone or pair it with a monitor arm depending on your setup needs. A riser works well if you want to elevate your entire keyboard and monitor together, while an articulating monitor arm gives you more flexibility to adjust height and angle independently—many remote workers use both for maximum ergonomic control.

    How much weight can a desk riser hold?

    Most quality desk risers can safely support 50-150 pounds depending on the brand and materials, which is sufficient for dual monitors, keyboards, and desk accessories. Always check the manufacturer's weight capacity before purchasing to ensure it supports your specific setup, and distribute weight evenly across the riser surface.
