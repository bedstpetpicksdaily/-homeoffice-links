The market offers dozens of options, from monitor arms to anti-fatigue mats to desk cooling solutions. Some prioritize adjustability and durability; others focus on budget-friendly setups or factory-direct pricing. This guide compares the major accessory categories you'll encounter, their genuine trade-offs, and which options work best for different summer work scenarios.

When selecting a monitor arm for a standing desk setup, verify that it has a weight capacity exceeding your monitor's actual weight by at least 20% and check for smooth, friction-based adjustment mechanisms rather than spring-loaded ones, which can drift downward over months of daily height changes.

Editor's Tip

Ergonomic Monitor Arms

Monitor arms solve a foundational problem: screen position. A quality arm allows you to position your screen at true eye level, reducing neck and shoulder strain during long standing sessions. Look for models with multiple pivot points (horizontal, vertical, and rotational) so you can adapt as you shift between sitting and standing throughout the day. Summer benefit: adjustable arms also tilt to combat screen glare from windows or bright outdoor light.

Best for: Anyone spending 6+ hours daily at a standing desk; essential if you experience neck or shoulder tension by end-of-day

Anti-Fatigue Mats with Cooling Properties

Standing in one spot for hours puts strain on feet, calves, and lower back—discomfort compounds in summer heat. Anti-fatigue mats add cushioning and texture to encourage subtle micro-movements that improve blood circulation. Cooling-specific mats address the summer-heat problem directly, helping reduce the psychological and physical fatigue that comes from standing on a warm floor all day. These typically feature textured surfaces that also improve grip and posture stability.

Best for: Remote workers standing 4+ hours daily in warm climates; especially valuable if you have a home office in direct sunlight or without air conditioning

Adjustable Keyboard and Mouse Trays

Keyboard trays keep wrists in a neutral position—a critical detail many remote workers ignore until RSI symptoms appear. Quality trays offer height and tilt adjustability so you can adapt to both sitting and standing positions without straining your wrists or elbows. Pairing a keyboard tray with a monitor arm creates a complete ergonomic setup; one without the other leaves you solving only half the problem. Summer-specific benefit: reduces the arm fatigue that intensifies when you're already warm and fatigued.

Best for: People with a history of wrist pain, carpal tunnel concerns, or those typing 4+ hours daily

Desk Lamps with Adjustable Color Temperature

Summer glare is a serious problem, especially in offices with windows or skylights. Adjustable-temperature desk lamps let you shift from cool light (5000K+, better for alertness) during peak heat hours to warmer light (3000K, easier on the eyes) as afternoon fatigue sets in. Quality models offer brightness adjustment independent of color temperature, so you can dial in exactly what your eyes need without creating additional heat in your workspace. This is genuinely different from a fixed desk lamp.

Best for: Offices with significant natural light variability; anyone experiencing afternoon eye strain or headaches during summer work

Footrests with Textured Surfaces

Footrests address a specific problem: when standing at a desk, feet typically dangle or rest flat on the ground, restricting circulation and increasing fatigue. A textured footrest encourages subtle weight shifts and provides ergonomic support. Unlike anti-fatigue mats (which you stand on), footrests are platforms you rest your feet on while standing—a different solution for people who can't use full-floor mats due to space constraints. The textured surface also prevents slipping when your feet get warm or sweaty during summer work.

Best for: Offices where full-size anti-fatigue mats aren't practical; standing desk users seeking targeted foot support without floor coverage

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Monitor arms and keyboard trays solve specific, measurable ergonomic problems: reduced neck strain, neutral wrist position, improved posture—backed by occupational health research
  • ✓ Anti-fatigue mats with cooling properties directly address summer-specific pain points: heat fatigue, prolonged standing discomfort, and psychological strain from warm work environments
  • ✓ Adjustable accessories (arms, keyboard trays, lamps) work with both sitting and standing positions, maximizing the value of your standing desk investment across different work modes
  • ✓ Factory-direct ergonomic furniture brands offer significant price advantages over retail alternatives without sacrificing build quality, making a complete accessory setup more affordable
Cons
  • ✗ Budget-conscious setups that skip keyboard trays or monitor arms to save money often result in wrist and neck pain later—the cheapest option upfront becomes expensive in medical costs
  • ✗ Cooling-specific anti-fatigue mats don't actually cool; they *feel* cooler through materials and texture, but won't solve heat problems in offices above 80°F without supplemental cooling (fans, AC)

Our Verdict

Best overall setup: Monitor arm + keyboard tray + anti-fatigue mat. This combination solves the three biggest ergonomic problems standing desk users face. The monitor arm prevents neck strain; the keyboard tray keeps wrists neutral; the anti-fatigue mat reduces lower-body fatigue and encourages healthy micro-movements. Summer-specific: add a temperature-adjustable desk lamp to combat glare and a desk fan to address heat fatigue—glare and heat are the primary productivity killers in warm-season home offices.

If budget is tight: Prioritize the monitor arm first. Neck and shoulder pain is the most common complaint among standing desk users and the hardest to fix later. A keyboard tray is your second priority. Anti-fatigue mats are third—valuable but less critical than proper screen and keyboard positioning.

For small spaces or renters: A footrest replaces a full anti-fatigue mat if floor space is limited. Pair it with the monitor arm and keyboard tray for the essential ergonomic foundation without requiring full-floor coverage.

Summer-specific recommendation: Don't skip the adjustable desk lamp or cooling anti-fatigue mat. Summer significantly amplifies fatigue from poor ergonomics. A cooling mat and proper lighting reduce eye strain, heat-related fatigue, and the afternoon productivity collapse that plagues warm-season remote workers. These aren't luxury upgrades—they're practical solutions to seasonal workplace challenges.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most important accessory if I can only buy one?

A monitor arm. Screen position is the foundation of desk ergonomics. Improper monitor height causes neck strain that compounds over months. A monitor arm is adjustable, solves a specific problem, and works with any standing desk. Everything else builds on this foundation.

Do I need a cooling anti-fatigue mat, or is a regular mat fine?

It depends on your climate and office setup. In climates above 78°F or offices without strong AC, a cooling mat (which uses moisture-wicking materials and textured surfaces) noticeably reduces the psychological fatigue of standing on a hot floor. In cooler climates or well-air-conditioned offices, a standard anti-fatigue mat works fine. The 'cooling' benefit is material-based comfort, not actual temperature reduction.

Can I use generic office accessories with any standing desk?

Mostly, yes—but compatibility matters. Monitor arms attach via VESA mounts (standard across most desks). Keyboard trays typically clamp to desk edges; measure your desk depth and thickness first. Anti-fatigue mats are universal. The key: verify dimensions and attachment methods before purchasing. Factory-direct brands like Sunaofe design their accessories to work seamlessly with their desks, reducing compatibility headaches.

How much desk space do these accessories actually require?

A monitor arm uses minimal desk footprint—just the base. A keyboard tray clamps underneath, freeing desktop space. An anti-fatigue mat needs full floor coverage (typically 20x30 inches minimum). A footrest occupies less floor space than a full mat. If desk space is tight, prioritize a monitor arm and keyboard tray; use a footrest instead of a full mat.

Should I transition accessories between sitting and standing positions, or leave them in one place?

Adjustable accessories (good monitor arms, keyboard trays) should move with you. The whole point is supporting proper posture in both positions. Set your monitor arm and keyboard tray for your standing height, then adjust them down if you sit. This takes 30 seconds and prevents the ergonomic benefit from becoming a liability in the other position. Fixed accessories defeat the purpose of a convertible standing desk.
