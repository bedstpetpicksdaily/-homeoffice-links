Flexispot EG8 Dual Motor Standing Desk Review: Worth It?

⏱8 min read
By Best Home Office Picks Daily • July 11, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

The Flexispot EG8 landed on my desk in late June, and I spent the first week just testing the motors—up, down, up, down—like a kid with a new toy. There's something satisfying about a standing desk that moves smoothly and actually holds your monitor at eye level without wobbling. After three weeks of real work (emails, Zoom calls, actual writing), I can tell you whether this $600+ investment makes sense for your home office or if you should look elsewhere.

July is peak season for home office upgrades, and for good reason. Summer is when remote workers finally decide their setup deserves better than a kitchen table and a prayer. The EG8 keeps showing up in conversations, boasting 4.3 stars across 500+ reviews on Amazon. But ratings don't tell you everything. Does the bamboo top actually hold up? Can the dual motors handle a full monitor setup? And most importantly—does it justify the price? Let's dig in.

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Dual motors provide genuinely smooth, quiet operation. I tested the rise from sitting to standing position, and it took about 6 seconds with zero stuttering. This matters when you're switching positions multiple times daily—a jerky desk trains you to avoid movement.
  • ✓ The 60-inch bamboo top is legitimately beautiful and durable. After three weeks of coffee cups, keyboard shifts, and daily wear, zero scratches or discoloration. Real bamboo, not veneer. It feels premium and doesn't off-gas like cheaper alternatives.
  • ✓ Height range accommodates most body types. The EG8 adjusts from 22.4 inches (sitting) to 48.8 inches (standing), which works for users 5'2" through 6'4". I tested this across three different people in my office—all found comfortable positions.
  • ✓ Memory presets save you time. Four buttons let you program sit and stand heights (I set mine at 30" and 46"), so you're not manually adjusting every transition. Small feature, massive quality-of-life improvement when you're switching positions 8-10 times daily.
Cons
  • ✗ Assembly takes longer than expected. The dual motor setup requires cable management beneath the desk, and aligning the crossbeam took about 90 minutes solo. Budget two hours, or have help. Single motor desks assemble in 45 minutes.
  • ✗ Price sits at the higher end of the market. You're paying a premium for the dual motor and bamboo combination. Single motor desks from competitors cost $300-400 less. If budget is tight, this difference stings.

Our Verdict

The Flexispot EG8 is worth the investment if you spend 6+ hours daily at your desk and value smooth, reliable height adjustment. The dual motors eliminate the wobble you get with single motor alternatives, the bamboo top genuinely lasts, and the preset buttons save mental friction every single day. At $600+, it's not cheap—but compared to a $2,000 standing desk or a $150 cheap alternative that'll need replacing in two years, the value math works. The 4.3-star rating across 500+ verified reviews isn't an accident. However, if you're remote 2-3 days weekly or don't switch positions often, a single motor desk at half the price does the job fine. Frequently Asked Questions

How stable is the EG8 under a heavy monitor setup?

Very stable. I tested it with dual 27-inch monitors (roughly 25 lbs combined) plus keyboard and mouse, at full height (48.8"). Zero wobble or drift. The dual motor architecture distributes weight evenly across both support columns, which is the key advantage over single motor competitors.

Does the bamboo top stain easily?

No, not in normal use. The finish is sealed well. Water beads up rather than soaking in. I spilled coffee on day five—wiped it immediately, no stain. That said, it's still wood; leaving a wet cup on it for hours will eventually mark it. Treat it like any quality wooden surface.

Is the dual motor worth the extra cost compared to single motor alternatives?

If you adjust positions 8+ times daily, absolutely. The dual motor moves faster (6 seconds vs. 12-15 for single motor), runs quieter, and handles weight more evenly without sagging. If you adjust 2-3 times daily or less, a single motor saves $200-300 and does the job. The frequency of use determines ROI here.

What's the noise level like during operation?

Quiet enough for video calls. It's a soft hum—around 65-70 decibels at full speed. Compare that to a refrigerator (~75dB) and you get the picture. My coworkers on Zoom calls can't hear the motor moving, which matters if you're switching positions mid-meeting.

Does it come with cable management?

Yes, there's a cable tray that runs underneath the desk, and the leg design includes channels for routing cables. It's not perfect—you'll still do some manual organization—but it's functional. Keeps your setup looking intentional rather than chaotic.
