What to Look For

Height Range and Adjustability

Look for risers that adjust from at least 4-16 inches in height. This range accommodates both sitting and standing positions across different desk heights. Smooth, tool-free adjustment mechanisms (gas springs or quick-release levers) are crucial—if it's hard to adjust, you won't use it. Bonus: some models offer preset height buttons so you can toggle between your ideal sitting and standing positions without fiddling.

Weight Capacity and Stability

Your riser needs to safely hold your monitor, keyboard, and any other desktop equipment. Look for models rated for at least 22-35 lbs, which covers most single or dual-monitor setups. A wide, weighted base is essential for stability—avoid flimsy designs that wobble when you adjust height or lean forward.

Workspace Surface Area

Measure what actually sits on your riser. A cramped surface forces you to choose between monitor and keyboard, defeating the purpose. Aim for at least 24" x 20" of usable space. Larger platforms (32"+ wide) are better if you have dual monitors or need room for a keyboard and mouse without overlap.

Portability and Build Quality

If you're buying a "portable" riser, it should actually move with you. Check the weight (ideally under 25 lbs), whether it folds or has a compact footprint, and if it comes with a carrying case. Materials matter too—steel frames are more durable than plastic, and they resist wobbling better over time. Budget models sometimes cut corners on stability to hit price points; read verified reviews specifically about long-term durability.
