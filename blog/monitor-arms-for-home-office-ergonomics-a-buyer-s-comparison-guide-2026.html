Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between monitor arms and monitor stands?

Monitor stands are fixed fixtures with limited adjustment. Monitor arms are mechanical mounting systems with full vertical, horizontal, and rotational movement, giving you complete control over positioning throughout the workday. Arms also free up more desk space since they mount to the desk edge or wall rather than occupying desktop real estate.

How do I know if a monitor arm will support my monitor?

Check three things: (1) Your monitor's weight in pounds—compare it to the arm's maximum weight capacity rating. (2) Your monitor's VESA pattern, typically 75x75mm or 100x100mm (found in the monitor's manual or specs). (3) The arm's reach distance from the desk clamp to the monitor center, which must extend far enough for your desk depth. If any spec doesn't match, the arm won't work safely or at all.

What's the ideal distance and height for a monitor?

Position your monitor so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level, and place it 20–26 inches from your eyes (roughly arm's length away). This minimizes neck strain and allows natural downward gaze, which is easier on the eyes than looking straight ahead. Adjust height and distance based on your chair height and desk depth.

Do monitor arms actually improve productivity?

Yes, when combined with proper positioning. Monitor arms eliminate neck and back strain, which reduces fatigue and allows longer focus periods. They also free desk space and enable flexible repositioning, supporting better work habits and fewer distractions. Studies show proper ergonomic setup, including monitor positioning, improves both comfort and concentration.

Should I buy a premium arm like Sunaofe or a cheaper VESA-compatible arm?

It depends on budget and use case. Premium arms ($400+) offer better locking mechanisms and build stability, making them ideal for 8+ hour daily use. Budget arms ($150–$250) work fine for smaller monitors and lighter use, but you must verify weight capacity and VESA compatibility carefully. A cheap arm that sags is a poor investment; a quality arm that holds position for years is cost-effective.
