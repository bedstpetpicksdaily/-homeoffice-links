What to Look For

  • Adjustable lumbar support with multiple depth settings – Your lower back has a natural curve, and the chair needs to match it. Look for chairs where you can adjust both the height and forward/backward depth of the lumbar support, not just a static built-in curve. This customization matters because everyone's spine is different, and what works at 9 AM might need tweaking by afternoon.
  • Seat depth and height that lets your feet flat on the floor – When you're working from home all day, poor seat geometry creates pressure on your thighs and throws off your entire posture. The seat should be deep enough to support your full thigh without cutting into the back of your knees, and adjustable so your feet rest flat (or on a footrest) with your knees at 90 degrees.
  • Breathable materials and recline function for shift work – Home office chairs often run hot because you're in them longer, and you might shift positions throughout the day. Look for mesh backing or breathable fabrics, plus a recline feature that lets you adjust tension so you can lean back briefly during calls without losing lumbar support.
