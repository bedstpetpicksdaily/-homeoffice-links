Frequently Asked Questions

How quiet is the motor really? I'm on Zoom calls constantly.

The single motor is genuinely quiet—reviewers compare it to a soft humming sound rather than a mechanical buzz. You won't hear it through your microphone unless you're sitting directly next to the frame during adjustment. For frequent video calls, this is a legitimate advantage over budget models that sound like someone's drilling near your desk.

Can I use any desktop surface with this frame, or does it require Uplift's own?

You can pair this frame with any quality desktop of 60" width that supports the weight. Most standard commercial-grade desktops work fine. Avoid extremely thin or hollow-core surfaces—they won't hold up long under the motor's adjustment stress. IKEA Gerton tops, hardwood boards, or commercial laminates all work. Just make sure your desktop weighs enough to balance the motor's torque.

Is the single motor powerful enough for a thick, heavy desktop plus equipment?

Yes, but there's a weight limit around 100-120 lbs depending on the specific model. Most setups with a desktop, monitor arm, and typical office gear land around 80-90 lbs, so you're in the safe zone. If you're planning a massive setup with multiple monitors, printers, and equipment on the desk surface itself, check the manufacturer's specs. Exceeding the weight capacity doesn't break the motor immediately—it just strains it and reduces lifespan.

How long does the height adjustment actually take from sitting to standing?

Count on 8-12 seconds to move through the full height range (usually 28-48 inches). It's not slow, but it's noticeably slower than dual-motor frames which can do the same in 4-6 seconds. For a single desk adjustment once or twice per day, you won't care. For someone switching every hour, the cumulative time adds up.

What's the warranty situation, and does it actually protect you?

Uplift typically offers a 7-year warranty on the frame and motor, which is above average for the category. The catch: warranties cover manufacturing defects, not wear-and-tear or accidents. If your motor dies in year 3 from normal use, you're covered. If you spill coffee into the motor or overload it, you're not. Read your specific warranty terms—they matter more than the length.
