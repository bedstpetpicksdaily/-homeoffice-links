Frequently Asked Questions

How stable is this desk really at full height?

Tested at 48.4" (maximum height) with two 27" monitors, a monitor arm, keyboard, and mouse pad—zero wobble or bounce. The dual motor system is the difference. Single-motor desks often develop sway at the extremes. Stability matters because an unstable desk breaks your concentration and creates safety issues if monitors are mounted above the surface.

Is the reclaimed wood finish worth the extra cost?

Yes, if durability and appearance matter to you. Reclaimed wood doesn't show dust accumulation like matte black surfaces do, and minor scratches blend in instead of becoming visible marks. Standard finishes chip and develop patina faster. The premium is 15-20% typically, and you'll avoid a desk replacement in 3-4 years.

Can you actually use the app controls, or is it just gimmicky?

The physical control panel works perfectly fine on its own. The app is genuinely optional. That said, if you set custom height presets (standing at 44", sitting at 28"), the app just remembers them so you don't reprogram after power loss. It's convenient, not essential. The desk functions fully without ever touching the app.

What's the real lifespan on the motors?

Autonomous covers motors for 5 years, and user reports suggest they last 7-10 years with normal use (5-10 height adjustments daily). The motors are industrial-grade, not the cheap ones in budget standing desks. Your biggest wear item will likely be the desktop surface or cable management if you're rough with it.

Is 72" really necessary or can I get away with 60"?

60" works if you're single-monitor or minimal desk real estate. At 72", you have genuine space to work—monitor, second monitor or tablet, notebook, coffee, phone—without overlap. If you're a minimalist, 60" saves $150-200. If you actually use your desk surface, the extra 12 inches justify the cost.
