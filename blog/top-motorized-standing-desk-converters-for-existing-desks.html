What to Look For

  • Weight capacity that exceeds your actual load: Don't just think about your monitor and keyboard—factor in a second monitor, desk lamp, files, and anything else you'll place on top. Look for converters rated for at least 25-30 pounds above what you estimate you'll actually use, since motors perform best when not operating at maximum capacity.
  • A sturdy, stable base that won't wobble: Because you're adding a motorized platform to an existing desk, stability becomes even more critical. Check reviews specifically mentioning wobble or tipping, and prioritize models with wide feet, cross-bracing, or anti-slip pads that work on your desk surface.
  • Dual motors for even weight distribution: Single-motor converters can struggle with uneven loads and may tilt slightly during the height transition. Dual-motor systems lift more evenly and maintain a perfectly level desktop, which matters especially if you're working with monitors or multiple screens.

  • Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I use a motorized standing desk converter on any desk?

    Most motorized desk converters work on standard desks, but you need to check weight capacity and desk surface dimensions. Make sure your desk is sturdy enough to support the converter plus your monitor, keyboard, and other equipment—most quality converters support 25-50 lbs. Measure your desk depth to ensure the converter won't overhang or interfere with your work setup.

    How much does a motorized standing desk converter cost?

    Motorized standing desk converters typically range from $200 to $800 depending on size, lift capacity, and brand. Budget options around $200-400 work well for light loads, while premium dual-motor converters with higher weight capacity run $600-800. Many companies offer them as wellness benefits, so check if your employer provides reimbursement.

    What's the difference between motorized and manual standing desk converters?

    Motorized converters use electric motors to raise and lower with a button or preset heights, while manual ones require manual cranking or adjustment. Motorized models are faster and easier to use throughout the day, encouraging more frequent position changes, but cost more and require a power outlet. Manual converters are budget-friendly and don't need electricity, but switching positions requires more effort.

    How long does it take for a standing desk converter to go up and down?

    Most quality motorized desk converters raise or lower at about 1-1.5 inches per second, taking 10-15 seconds to reach maximum height. Faster isn't always better—slower speeds are quieter and more stable for expensive equipment. Preset buttons let you save your sitting and standing heights for instant one-click adjustments.
