What to Look For

  • Lumbar support that targets the lower back: Look for chairs with adjustable lumbar support that can be positioned to support your natural L4-L5 curve. Sciatica typically originates from compression in this exact area, so a chair that allows you to customize lower back support is non-negotiable. The support should be firm but not rigid, allowing your spine to maintain its natural shape without forcing an unnatural posture.
  • Seat depth and cushioning that relieves nerve pressure: A seat that's too deep can put pressure on the back of your thighs and compress the sciatic nerve. Look for chairs with a seat depth between 16-17 inches and memory foam or high-density cushioning that's firm enough to support your weight without creating pressure points. The cushioning should distribute your weight evenly rather than creating a "hammock" effect that sags in the middle.

Choose a chair with a smooth recline mechanism and tension adjustment so you can shift between upright, slightly reclined, and tilted positions throughout your workday. The ability to change positions reduces compression on the sciatic nerve and prevents the stiffness that makes pain worse.
